## 2026-11-10 — NEXT: the demo is three city quests

**This is the demo.** The city is currently an endless free drive with sentries in it and no
reason to go anywhere. Three quests give it one. Nothing here is built yet; the shape and the
story are settled, the details marked OPEN are not.

### The canon this rests on — read before changing any of it

`assets/comic/COMIC_SCRIPT.md` and the 2026-11-06 story entry are the source. Short version:

- Everyone is **nameless**.
- **HIM:** a mechanic robot, an older model. A welding visor flipped up on his head, a leather
  apron, one rusted arm and one chrome. He built the war-car from scrap.
- **HER:** sleeker, newer, with **a square socket on her chest where an old data chip glows
  cyan**. The chip carries code the city needs. The **Collectors** (the armoured hunter cars)
  took her and keep her **intact**, because they don't know whether they can get the code out
  without breaking her.
- **The techno-lords** rule the neon city behind its walls. Everything that didn't make the cut
  was left outside, in the **Scrapheap**.
- **The Scrap Saints** are the rebels — they fight the lords, *or whoever controls them*. They
  don't care which; they pay bounty for any state property you wreck. Their line:
  *"We don't care who's holding the leash. We cut the leash."*
- Old models are beneath the guards' notice. The guards **only fear high-tech androids packed
  with powerful energy attacks** — that is the in-world reason the sentries ignore you.

**New (2026-11-10): her memory has been flashed.** She is intact, but she does not remember who
she is or what she carries. The chip is still in the socket — they daren't pull it. So rescuing
her does **not** answer anything, and "retrieve her memory" is a later quest, not this demo.

### Nobody knows what the techno-lords are. That is the point.

Three claims circulate in the city, and **the game never settles it**:

1. **The AI took power.** It overthrew the humans and rules in their place.
2. **The humans are still there.** The lords are a front; the hand is behind the curtain.
3. **The robots are their own faction now,** at war with humans because *the humans tried to
   destroy the robots first*.

Each claim explains the one fact the player has already *played* — "they hunt only new tech" —
a different way: the regime fears being outclassed / the humans fear a machine that could
refuse / the state is **conscripting** the best machines for a war that never ended.

The mechanic came first and the lore explains it three ways. Keep it that way: no quest resolves
it, and each one yields a single hard fact that fits more than one claim.

Cheap way to put the argument in the world: **graffiti on the building walls**, one slogan per
claim, placed by the same deterministic per-chunk trick as the sentries. The player reads the
argument off the city and no character has to explain it. The sentries never say what they
believe — they just stand there and let you walk past.

### The quest shape (decided)

**Radio briefs you; two locations per quest; gear first, job second.**

- **The radio gives the quest.** No dialogue system, no interiors, no talking heads needed — and
  it talks **while you drive**, which turns travel into the story beat instead of dead air. An NPC
  in a room can't follow you.
- **Location 1 is where the GEAR is.** Not a talker. The reason you must go is that the *object*
  is there. A location whose only content is text is the oldest bad quest design there is.
- **Location 2 is the job.**
- **PRACTICAL RULE: the gear site sits SHALLOWER than the target** (smaller `|y|`), so a quest is
  one continuous push deeper into the city and never a backtrack over ground you've cleared.
  This is just a number per site, so it is free — don't get it wrong anyway.
- The radio **drops a marker on the map** (`M`, `MAP_RADII`, city overview already exist). That is
  the objective UI, and it is diegetic rather than a HUD arrow.

**He is a mechanic.** Every gear job fits his profession — he cuts, he assembles, he drives. Use
that instead of inventing specialists.

### Quest 1 — RESCUE HER  (OPEN: the least decided of the three)

- **Nobody briefs you.** This one is not offered. The Saints would rather you hit the datacenter;
  the old model would rather you seal the bunker. You go anyway — it is the comic's last line,
  *"Hold on. I'm coming."*, and it is the only quest that defies both factions.
- **Location 2:** wherever they are holding her. OPEN.
- **The escape is the design.** The whole game teaches that nobody looks at you. The moment she is
  on your back **you are carrying new tech** and every sentry in the city can see you. The way out
  is the way in with the rule inverted — and that is `f.mad` / `foeMadN`, code that already ships.
  *"They never looked at me twice. They looked at her the whole way out."*
- **Gear — OPEN, and note the trap:** he already has a welding visor, so a cutting torch is not
  something he needs to buy. The gear has to be something he cannot improvise. Candidates: a
  **Collector transponder** so the grabbing arms read him as one of theirs; a **power cell**
  because the holding block is shielded and his torch won't bite; a **blank memory core** to catch
  whatever is left of her (which comes back empty — that is the hook for the later memory quest).
- **Fact it yields:** she is out, and she does not know who she is. She says one thing that fits
  none of the three claims cleanly, because she is the only one who has been inside.

### Quest 2 — THE DATACENTER  (a fight in a huge city square)

- **The Saints brief you** over the radio. It is their war aim: if claim 1 is true, the usurper
  lives in the machine.
- **Location 2 is a huge city square** — a plaza where the lords' physical plant is exposed, and
  **you have to fight something there**. Not a stealth bomb run.
- **The canon already wrote the boss.** The guards "only fear high-tech androids packed with
  powerful energy attacks". That is exactly what should be standing in the square: the thing even
  the lords' own guards are afraid of. The player has spent the whole city being ignored; here is
  the one machine that does not ignore him.
- **Location 1 — the supplier:** explosives **assembled** from parts bought separately (fuel,
  oxidiser, detonator). A bomb built from three purchases feels earned in a way a bought bomb
  doesn't, and the assembly is a mechanic's job. Reuses the store screen and `cash`.
- **Fact it yields:** the datacenter dies and **something keeps giving orders anyway.**

### Quest 3 — CEMENT THE BUNKER  (the best-shaped one — build it when you want a set-piece)

- **The radio: "there is a hidden bunker in the city, and we just got intel on that."** The intel
  comes from **an old model, and he is telling the truth** — he was there, he saw it. Read him
  narrowly: he is reliable **about the bunker**, not omniscient about who runs the state. "A human
  is alive in a hole" and "humans are still running things" are different statements. He knows the
  first. Nobody knows the second.
- **Location 1 is a yard, and you STEAL A CONCRETE MIXER.** This is the best first location in the
  game because it is not a waypoint, it is a transformation: you arrive in the war-car and leave
  driving a slow, heavy truck. It is the only quest that changes how driving *feels*.
- **Location 2:** the bunker's air intake. You back the mixer up and **pour**.
- **You do not kill the last human.** You seal him in and drive away. Very robot, and the best of
  the three endings: *he wanted the world to wait for him. Now it can.*
- **Fact it yields:** the air handlers were **already running on a timer** — nobody inside has
  touched anything for decades.
- **Cost: this is the expensive one.** A second drivable vehicle with its own handling.

### The chirp language (his idea, 2026-11-10)

Dialogue should **sound** like the machine-to-machine language — R2-D2-ish chirps — while the
text types out. This is nearly free and it fits machinery that already exists:

- The comic **already types captions** at `COMIC.TYPE_SPEED` letters/second. Fire one chirp per
  revealed character and the speech paces itself off the existing typewriter.
- `Snd` is fully synthesised WebAudio with `blip(freq, dur, type, vol, slide)` and
  `noise(dur, vol, cutStart, cutEnd)` already in hand. Derive the pitch from the character code so
  a given line always chirps the same way, add a slide for the warble, and skip the chirp on
  spaces and punctuation so it phrases.
- Give each speaker a pitch band and a waveform so the Saints, the old model and the radio are
  distinguishable without a portrait. She should sound different from everyone.

### Engineering notes

- **The city has no unique buildings at all.** It is infinite and procedural (`makeCityChunk`,
  `cityRng`, `hash3`). Placing sites is the **same problem as the sentries**, and `foeChunkPlan` is
  the template: deterministic, keyed, materialised within a radius.
- The one difference: sites are **unique and must appear exactly once at a known depth**, so they
  want a fixed table — `CITY_SITES = [{kind, atY, ...}]` — not a random draw.
- **`makeCityChunk` has to reserve a `core`** for a site, or a procedural building will grow on
  top of it. This is the first real change to the city generator.
- "Drive deeper to reach your objective" also gives the city the **progression it currently
  lacks**. Gear sites shallow, targets deep (the practical rule above).
- The radio wants a tiny queue: a line, a speaker, a chirp band, and a map marker.

### Before building any of it — test the traversal

More locations is only better if **crossing the city is already fun**. Drive 2,000 units across it
and ask whether you'd want to do that twice per quest. If the answer is no, the fix is in the city,
not in the quest structure, and two locations per quest would make it worse. Ten-minute test; do it
first.

### Open decisions

- The rescue: where she is held, and what the gear is (see the trap above).
- What is standing in the square, in detail.
- Whether the three quests are **pick one per run** (three endings, replay) or all three in one
  run in any order. Not settled.
- Whether the graffiti ships with the demo or later.

## 2026-11-10 — Camera shake fell off a cliff; sentry density settled at MEAN 5

Two things, both from winding the sentry density up and then back down.

### The shake bug: `explode()` had no distance term, and never had

He played it and reported "the screen is shaking a lot". `explode()` is the only thing that kicks
the camera, and it did so at **full strength from anywhere in the world**:

```js
cam.shake = Math.min(9, cam.shake + power*3);   // no distance term at all
```

That was invisible as a bug for the whole life of the game, because explosions were rare and
always happened under your own bumper. The sentries broke it: `foeDie` calls `explode` and hunter
cars cruise at `CITY_CRUISE` 115, well over `FOE.RAM_MIN` 40, so **they mow sentries down all over
the city** — and the 2D pane only shows ±160 × ±90 world units, so almost none of it is on screen.

Measured at MEAN 25, driving and **never firing a shot**: 3 deaths in 30 s, **all three further
than 600 units away**, screen shaking 13% of frames. Firing: 27 deaths, 25 off screen, 27% of
frames, average `cam.shake` 0.89 and pegged near the cap of 9 two thirds of the time.

A sentry's blast was also rated `1.2` — **identical to a whole car exploding** (barrel 0.8,
missile 1.0, mine 1.1, car 1.2, car bomb 2.4).

**The fix** — `SHAKE` in config.js §7b, and `shakeFade(x, y)` in index.html:

- Full kick inside `SHAKE.NEAR` 110, linear to **zero at `SHAKE.FAR` 700**.
- `SHAKE.SENTRY` 0.45 instead of 1.2. One person popping is a bump (1.35 kick, 0.1 s), not a bomb.
- A blast **beside** you is untouched: car bomb at your feet still 7.20, car wreck still 3.60.

After: 4% of frames while driving, 9% while firing — in a run that had **more** deaths (35 vs 3;
hunter AI varies a lot run to run, so compare rates, not single runs).

### The booms too (his call: "if it is offscreen don't add the boom sfx")

`Snd.boom()` takes no arguments — one fixed-volume bang regardless of distance — so it is gated on
the **same** `shakeFade`, which keeps exactly one definition of "too far to matter" for both the
kick and the bang. Past `SHAKE.FAR` you get neither.

Safe because **nothing the player fires can reach that far**: a missile is 190 units/s for 2.6 s
= **494 units**, mines land behind you, barrels are beside you. So it can only ever silence an
explosion someone else caused, off screen. **That bound is now a test** — raise missile range past
`SHAKE.FAR` and the suite fails instead of the game quietly going deaf.

### Density: settled at MEAN 5 (and the city-centre set is on record)

Swept to 10× and back. **Every number in `FOE_POP` has to move together** — this is the trap:

- `SD` is in the **same units as MEAN**. At MEAN 2.5, SD 0.8 is a 33% spread and blocks really
  differ; leave SD at 0.8 and raise MEAN to 25 and it is a **3% spread** — every block gets exactly
  25 and "some blocks busy, some empty" is gone, replaced by a flat rate.
- `MAX` is a ceiling, not a second knob, and only does something while it is being hit. It stops
  mattering past about `MEAN + 3*SD`; below that it quietly caps MEAN.
- **`ROADSIDE` moves OPPOSITE to MEAN.** Low MEAN needs it high or you meet nobody (0.6 at MEAN
  2.5); high MEAN needs it low or the frontage packs shoulder to shoulder and the city reads as a
  guard of honour lining the route. Compare `.shots/extreme-mean-25.png` (0.6) with
  `.shots/extreme-roadside-0.2.png` (0.2) — same 377 sentries, completely different city.
- New: **`FOE_POP.GAP` 16**. At MEAN 25 sentries were being placed **inside each other** (closest
  pair 0 units; already only 4 at MEAN 2.5, because `foeSeparate` only separates *provoked* ones).
  `foeSpot` now rejects a spot too near an already-placed one, which also makes density
  **self-limiting**: a block that runs out of room just skips that sentry.

**Now: `MEAN 5, SD 1.6, MAX 12, ROADSIDE 0.45`** → 148 live, nearest 200, 14.3 within 600,
28 in a good look.

**The city-centre set, measured and kept** for when the centre gets its own density:
`MEAN 25, SD 8, MAX 60, ROADSIDE 0.2` → ~377 live, ~190 in one view, nearest ~180, **0.28 ms of a
16.7 ms frame**. CPU is not what breaks at 10×; the distribution and the overlaps are.

Both sweep tables live in comments beside the knobs in config.js, with a note that rows are only
comparable **at equal `ROADSIDE`** — the MEAN 5 row has twice the bodies of the MEAN 3.5 row but
*fewer* within 300 units, purely because a smaller share stands on the frontage.

### Tests and one harness trap worth knowing

`node tools/selftest_foe.js` — **120 checks** (108 before; 12 new across the shake and the booms).
Plus `selftest_boost.js` 30 and `selftest_pad.js` 60.

**The harness trap:** `last = performance.now()` is the real clock, and `headless.js` ticks from
`t = 0`. If the match is already in `state === 'play'` on the **first** tick, `dt` comes out at
about **−1.8e9 seconds**, `acc` goes permanently negative and **`step()` never runs again** — the
game looks alive and simulates nothing. **Tick once before starting the match** (the title branch
returns early and resets `last`). `selftest_foe.js` gets away with it only because earlier heads
tick while the state is still `title`.

Second trap, same session: in the city, `keys['ArrowUp']` drove the car **north, back out through
the gate**, and `toggleOnFoot()` left the walker at the origin — 14,027 units from the nearest
sentry. A 40 s "soak in the city" was measuring empty wasteland. Pin the position and face
`-Math.PI/2`, and assert the sentry count mid-run.

## 2026-11-09 — Sentries in the city, with their own AI

**Start here.** The plan below is what this was built from; everything under that is older and
done. Built, checked, and **playtested — which is the only reason it works**: the first cut
passed 103 checks and put seventeen sentries in the city that the player could not see at all.
Read *the one that got away* before touching the placement.

### The rules, as decided. Do not re-litigate these.

| rule | where it lives |
|---|---|
| scattered all over the city, by density, not spawned at a point | `citySentries` / `foeSeedChunk`, foe.js |
| each one is on its own; shooting one does not turn the others on you | `foeDamage` touches only `f` |
| they ignore you until you shoot them (the comic's rule) | `foeThink` returns at once unless `f.mad` |
| being shot is what provokes one — that one | `foeDamage`, and **only when `ownerId === 0`** |
| a provoked one FOLLOWS you and **never shoots** | `foeThink`; there is no foe weapon anywhere |
| five of your pistol shots kill one | `FOE.HP` 90 against `PISTOL.DMG` 18 |
| the car's weapons kill one too | `updateShots`, `explode`, `foeRam` |
| it gives up when you get far enough away | `FOE_AI.GIVE_UP` + `LOSE_T` |

**The open question was asked and answered: it never shoots.** The brief said "they just follow
you for now" and a later answer said it chases the car "and shoots it as well"; asked which, the
user chose **follows only, never shoots — in the car or out of it**. So there is no sentry
weapon in the code at all, and adding one is a new decision, not a gap to be filled in.

### Where the knobs are

**`config.js` §3e** — two blocks, both new:

- **`FOE_AI`** — `CHASE_SPD 52` (he walks 46, runs 92, so a sentry out-paces a stroll and loses a
  sprint — that is the whole reason `GIVE_UP` means anything), `TURN 3.2`, `NEAR 170`, `STOP 18`,
  `GIVE_UP 900`, `LOSE_T 1.2`, `STUCK_T/STUCK_D`.
- **`FOE_POP`** — **superseded: see the 2026-11-10 density entry for the settled values.** As
  first built: `MEAN 0.22` sentries per block, `SD 0.80`, `MAX 3`, `SEED 4231`, `KEEP 3000`,
  `DROP 4200`, `TRIES 8` (no `GAP` yet).

**`foe.js`** — `FOE.HP 90`, `FOE.HIT_R 8` (the pistol's tolerance on a sentry; `PISTOL.HIT_R` 14
is a car's number), `FOE.SHOT_R 9`, `FOE.RAM 0.9` / `RAM_MIN 40`, `FOE_HIT_LIFT 0.5`.

### The one that got away: they were there, and they were invisible

Worth more than the rest of this entry, because every check passed and the feature did not work.
The user playtested it and said: *"I tried both index and the gunlab and I see no sentries."*

**What was wrong, in two layers.**

1. **The live set was a band of chunks, not a radius.** A chunk is 1400 long but the city's whole
   **4000-unit width**. The player only ever occupies the highway corridor at x≈0, so giving a
   body to a whole chunk spent **20 of 26 sentries three to four thousand units out to the side**,
   in blocks nobody will ever walk down. 5 of 26 were inside the 1500-unit billboard cull; **1**
   was inside 600 units.
2. **And the deeper one: a block has four pavement edges, and only ONE faces the highway.**
   `foeSpot` picked uniformly, so three sentries in four stood on interior side streets. This is
   why the obvious fix did not work — **raising the density changed nothing**. Measured:
   `MEAN 0.22` → 7 live, 1 inside 600; `MEAN 0.8` → 10 live, **still 1 inside 600**. The highway
   is a 360-wide corridor with no blocks in it and only one or two blocks within 600 units at all,
   so per-block density cannot put anybody near the road however high you wind it.

**The fix is `FOE_POP.ROADSIDE` (0.6)** — that share stand on their block's road-facing edge, the
rest keep to the back streets so it does not read as a guard of honour lining the route — plus
`KEEP`/`DROP` as a **radius** (1800/2400) instead of a chunk band. Nearest sentry on arrival went
from 485 units to **183**, and sentries met on a 32-second walk from **1 to 6**.

**Three lessons, in the order they cost time:**

- **"It exists in the array" is not "it is in the game."** Every check passed because every check
  asked whether the sentry was *placed, drawn, and culled correctly*. None asked whether the
  player would ever be near one. There are now three that do, under *the regression that bit* —
  nearest-under-400 and the road-facing share. If those fail, the enemies are unmeetable again.
- **A screenshot with the subject teleported in front of the camera proves nothing.** The original
  shots moved the nearest sentry to 78 units to get a good picture of it. That is the right shot
  for judging the *art* and worthless for judging the *placement*, and it is what let this ship.

### How many there actually are — measured, not guessed

**`FOE_POP.MEAN` is the knob** — the only number that changes how many there are. Swept along
the road and averaged over four spots (`live` = bodies inside `KEEP`, `view` = most visible in
one first-person look). The same table is in config.js beside the knob:

| MEAN | MAX | live | nearest | <300 | <600 | in one view |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.4 | 3 | 15 | 256 | 1.3 | 2.3 | 7 |
| 0.8 | 3 | 20 | 293 | 1.3 | 3.0 | 8 |
| 1.2 | 4 | 24 | 277 | 0.8 | 2.8 | 11 |
| **2.5** | **6** | **60** | **249** | **3.3** | **8.0** | **23** | *(set here)* |
| 3.5 | 8 | 80 | 190 | 5.0 | 11.0 | 28 |

**Two things the table will not tell you unless you look at it.** Below about **1.2 the curve is
flat and noisy** — at those rates the handful of blocks near the road dominate and luck decides,
which is why 0.4 and 0.8 both give ~1 sentry inside 300 units. And **`MAX` is the real ceiling**:
leave it at 3 and winding `MEAN` past ~1.5 does nothing, because every block clamps and the knob
stops answering. Raise them together.

`ROADSIDE` is the other half and is not interchangeable with `MEAN`: it decides *whether you ever
meet* the sentries, where `MEAN` only decides how many exist. Winding `MEAN` up with `ROADSIDE`
at 0 just fills the back streets — that is exactly the failure above. The clamp at nought also
lifts the realised rate above `MEAN`, since the normal's whole left half lands on "none".

**Cost at MEAN 2.5** (41 alive at the arrival spot), out of a 16.7 ms frame: `updateFoes` 0.004,
`citySentries` 0.014, `foeRam` 0.0005, `gatherBillboards` 0.019 — **0.04 ms the lot**. Even the
pathological case of every sentry provoked at once is 1.5 ms. Density is not what will cost
frames here; the per-sentry work is all O(n) over small n and the sheets are shared, so count is
very nearly free.

`devMode` (I) prints `SENTRIES n` — and `ON YOU n` in red when any are provoked — on the on-foot
HUD, so all of this can be turned by looking rather than by guessing.

### What is reused, and the one thing that is not

- **The flow field is the hunters' own** (`FLOW`/`flowNext`), so a sentry comes down the street
  that leads to you instead of grinding into a wall. It is built with *car* clearances; the only
  cost of borrowing it is that a sentry keeps to the roadway. A pedestrian variant is still a
  later nicety.
- **One line of `cityStep` had to change.** The field was only refilled while a hunter car was
  interested in you, which on foot is never. A provoked sentry hunting you on foot is the one
  thing that needs it with no car to ask — hence `foeMadN`, counted in `updateFoes` and read by
  `cityStep`.
- **Planning and materialising are separate** (`foeChunkPlan` / `citySentries`). The per-block
  normal draw is done once per chunk and cached — deterministic from `(chunk, side)` alone, so
  the same stretch of city always holds the same sentries. What gets a *body* is then decided by
  distance. That split is what lets the scatter stay "all over the city" while the live set stays
  small and near you. `foeLive` is the index of who has a body; `clearFoes` clears it, or the
  spawner would think sentries it can no longer see are still out there and never bring them back.
- **`foeDamage` / `foeDie` / `foeCollide` are NOT `damage()` / its death branch / `walkCollide`.**
  Each says why at its own definition. `damage()` is car-only the whole way down (`untouchable`,
  `armorCut`, respawn, deaths, the pit clip, the bounty); its death branch is the tanque's bomb,
  `lastHitBy`, `frags`, `convoyKills`. `foeDie` takes the reusable core the user asked for —
  `explode()` plus two `particles()` bursts in the sentry's colours — and nothing else.
- **A sentry's own death blast does not chain.** `explode` now has a foes loop, and it reads
  `dmg` **raw** rather than through the cars' old `dmg||30` fallback. Two things turn on that: a
  wrecked sentry passes 0 on purpose and must not splash its neighbour, and a 0-damage hit must
  not *provoke* one either, which would quietly break "no coordination". The cars' line is
  untouched, so nothing about car wrecks changed.

### Four things I did that nobody asked for

Each is small, each is flagged here because it is a decision and not an instruction:

1. **Sentries are solid** (`foeBlock`). A follow-only enemy that you can walk through is not a
   threat, it is a ghost. It does **not** hurt you by touching you — it simply will not let you
   past. Deliberately **not** inside `walkCollide`: `toggleOnFoot` uses `walkCollide` as a "is

## 2026-11-09 — The sentry walks: a rig on the voxel model

**Goal:** a walk cycle without drawing one. The faces are split into groups by where
they sit in the model, each group is rotated about a pivot, and the result is baked like any
other frame. Knobs: **`FOE_RIG` in config.js §3d**. Legs only — the arms are welded to the torso
by the carve, so an "arm" group would drag the chest with it.

**One posing function, both cameras.** `posedMesh(mesh, pose)` returns a new face list; both
`bakeFoeSheet` (first person) and `bakeFoe2D` (top-down) loop over poses and call it. There is no
per-view animation code, and that is the whole reason to pose the mesh rather than the sprite.
A face is assigned to a group by its CENTRE, so a quad never splits across a joint.

**The knee is load-bearing, not decoration.** With straight legs the cycle is a pure scissor:
`sin()` sampled at `POSES` points gives mirror-image duplicates, so 6 poses are really 3 shapes.
Bending the knee **only on the leg swinging forward** (`K*max(0, cos(ph))`) breaks that symmetry.
The selftest asserts all six poses are distinct shapes — that check is what stops someone
"simplifying" the knee away and quietly halving the animation.

**The body's dip is derived, not tuned.** Legs rotating about a hip lift the feet, so the body has
to come down or the sentry walks on tiptoe and bobs the wrong way. Rather than add a BOB constant,
`foeDrop()` puts a foot through the same transform and reads its height off. Pose 0 is exactly the
carve, feet at z=0. What is left is ≤0.65 units of a rotating foot BOX's corner under the road —
0.03 of the model's height, sub-pixel at every size this draws at. Tested, not fixed: an ankle
group would cost a tearing seam for an invisible gain.

**Poses bake lazily, one at a time** (`foeSheetEnsure`), and are **warmed one strip per frame**
once anything is walking (`foeWarm`, called from `updateFoes`). **Note for whoever measures this
next: you cannot time it in headless Chrome** — `--virtual-time-budget` freezes the clock, so
synchronous work always reads 0 ms. The numbers here come from node with the stubbed canvas,
which over-states Chrome; the project's own figure is ~30 ms for a 64-frame car sheet.

### The first-walk stutter, and what it cost (reported from play)

Lazy baking put a **387 ms strip bake on the frame where each pose was first shown** — six times
per view, in the middle of the walk, which is the worst possible moment. The work is
`YAWS x rows x faces` and is vertex-bound (the frames are 15x26 and 23x43; fill is nothing).
Two of those three multipliers were waste:

- **The flash row was dead weight.** Nothing can damage a sentry yet, so half of every bake drew
  a white silhouette that is never shown. Gone - `foeRow` is now one strip per pose. When damage
  arrives, prefer `hitFrame()`-style recolour at draw time, which is what the ASCII pane already
  does for cars, over baking the row back.
- **32 yaws is a car's number.** A car turns constantly and is read at speed; a sentry turns
  rarely and is 9 character rows tall. `FOE_RIG.YAWS` is **16** - 22.5-degree steps, invisible at
  this size.

**387 ms -> 96 ms a strip.** Then `foeWarm` moves the remaining five strips to the frames right
after a sentry starts moving (verified: all six done within six frames) instead of stuttering
through the cycle. If it still bites, the knobs in order of bluntness are `FOE_RIG.YAWS`,
`FOE_RIG.POSES`, and switching `FOE_VOX` to `sentry@64` (2008 faces against 4520).

### A worse bug found while investigating, which was NOT the cause

`bakeFoe2D` keyed its cache on the live `S`. `S` is constant in the city and through the run
stage, which is why this never fired - but **the bend eases the zoom frame by frame**, and there
it would have rebuilt all six strips every frame. `bakeCarSprites` has always avoided this by
pinning `S` to `homeZoom()` and letting `drawCar` scale by `zk`; the foe sheet now does the same,
and a selftest asserts that changing `S` does not rebuild it.

**The phase runs on DISTANCE, not time** (`f.phase += d/FOE_RIG.STRIDE`), so the feet don't skate
when the walk speed changes later. There is no AI: `f.walk` makes a sentry step forward and **Y**
toggles it on every sentry, which is how you look at the rig.

**The weakness, and it is the common case.** Seen side-on or three-quarter the walk reads well.
Seen HEAD-ON it barely moves — legs scissoring fore/aft are almost invisible from the front. That
is physically honest and completely wrong for a game, where the enemy walking straight at you is
what you will look at most. Candidate fixes, none tried: a little lateral sway, a slight lateral
splay on the legs, or arm swing once an enemy is drawn with its arms clear of its body.

**Correction worth keeping:** raising the voxel resolution does NOT separate the arms. The carve
walks each row and finds runs of non-background pixels; arms touching the torso are one run and
get one capped depth, at any resolution. What fixes it is a gap of background in the drawing, or
teaching the carve to split runs at the drawn black outline.

**Verified:** `node tools/selftest_foe.js` → 47/47 (pose 0 is the untouched carve, all poses are
distinct, feet exactly on the ground when standing, nothing sinks, the body dips rather than
rises, phase advances by exactly distance/STRIDE and wraps, the pose index stays inside the
sheet). Chrome: a 6-pose × 4-yaw contact sheet, and six sentries in the lab each frozen at a
different phase.

## 2026-11-09 — One drawing -> a voxel model: sprite_voxelize.py

**Goal:** stop paying for enemy directions in drawings. The cars get 32 angles free because
`bakeFpsSprites` renders them from a mesh; this makes a hand-drawn enemy do the same from ONE
front view. **It works, and the result is honestly worse-looking than the flat sprite** — see the
trade at the end before building on it.

**`tools/sprite_voxelize.py`** — `sentry_idle.png` → `assets/enemies/sentry-vox.js`
(`SPRITEVOX["sentry@64"]`, the same shape `car_voxelize.py` emits, so `voxMesh()` eats it
unchanged). Current build: `--res 64 --round 4.0`, 2008 faces, **68 KB**.

**How it guesses the depth it was never given.** A real visual hull needs two silhouettes to
intersect; with one picture an honest hull is a flat slab. So for every row of the sprite it takes
each horizontal RUN of solid pixels and gives it a superelliptical cross-section of depth
`min(width, cap)`. A narrow run — arm, leg, head — comes out round. A wide run — chest, shoulders
— is capped and comes out a rounded slab. That one rule is most of a humanoid.

**Three things it had to learn the hard way, all still in the code as comments:**

- **The drawn outline became a black skin.** A voxel's side faces take the colour of the column
  they sit behind, so the sprite's bold black outline painted the entire left and right of the
  model black — the first carve was a dark slab from every angle but dead ahead. `--erode 3`
  reassigns `dark` voxels within 3 of the silhouette to the nearest painted role; interior dark
  (the joints) is left alone, because that is real shading.
- **A standing figure gets no light.** `LIGHT` is `(-0.42,-0.58,0.70)`: fine for a car, whose lit
  faces are its horizontal tops, but a standing figure shows the camera its **+y** faces and
  LIGHT's y is negative, so every face you look at bakes at the flat 0.44 ambient. `FOE_LIFT`
  (1.9, a `brightness()` filter on the sheet, not on the flash row) puts it back.
- **Near-white washes out.** `accent` is `hexShade(col, 1.32)`, so a white body clamps accent to
  flat grey and the model loses all hue. `FOE_COL` is `#a8b6d4` — cool and well below white — so
  the highlight stays blue and the amber visor is the only warm thing on the model, which is what
  keeps it winning its ASCII cell.

**Also:** `--smooth 2` majority-filters the roles (the drawing holds far more detail than 36
voxels across can carry, and it aliases into salt-and-pepper), with `lamp` exempt so the visor is
never voted away. `scipy` here is built against numpy 1.x and will not import, so the erosion and
the filter are plain numpy — don't reintroduce the scipy dependency.

**Game side:** `foe.js` gained `foeMesh()`, `bakeFoeSheet()` (32 frames + a flash row, `vsc=1` so
one sheet pixel is one world unit, feet anchored near the frame bottom) and `FOE_RENDER`.
**X toggles voxel ↔ flat picture in place** — that comparison is the whole point of the feature,
keep it. Meshes and sheets are keyed by `SPRITEVOX` name and `spawnFoe(x, y, a, vox)` takes one,
so two carves (or two enemies) can stand side by side; the lab plants `sentry@96` down the left
and `sentry@64` down the right at four distances.

**`bakeFoe2D` / `drawFoe2D`: the same mesh in the DRIVING view.** A billboard only exists inside
the raycaster, so at first a sentry simply vanished when you got back in the car — reported from
play, not caught by a test. The fix is the second payoff of voxelising: the top-down pane is the
same mesh through `rasterCar` at the axonometric `KY`/`KZ` with `vsc = S`, which is what
`bakeCarSheet` does for a car. Baked at `S` and dropped by the same rescale that clears `CAR2D`
(`clearFoeSheets()`), and pushed into the painter's-algorithm `drawables` list on `y`, so sentries
sort correctly against cars and buildings. **The flat sprite could never have done this** — it is
drawn face-on from eye level and has no top-down view at all.

`FOE_LIFT_2D` is 1.4, against 1.9 for first person: the driving camera is only 38° up, so you
still see mostly the figure's vertical faces and only the shoulders and head catch `LIGHT`'s +z.
Unlifted they read as dark smudges beside the car.

**Note for anyone reading a screenshot comparison:** `asciiBlit` is called in exactly two places,
the gunner pane and the walk view. **The top-down driving view is never ASCII.** "Pixel vs ASCII"
is a first-person question only; `G` cycles `asciiMode` 0 → 1 → 2.

**The trade, which is the finding.** Side by side in the lab, at 95 units:

| | flat `sentry_idle.png` | the voxel carve |
|---|---|---|
| looks | crisp, coloured, visor and chest lights read | grey-blue, mushy, washed out |
| turning | always faces you | 32 real angles |
| per enemy | 1 drawing per state × per direction | 1 drawing, ever |
| ASCII | solid `@` block | still reads; slightly busier |

The voxel version **rotates and the flat one cannot**, and that is the only reason to prefer it.
It does not yet look as good, and the gap is tuning, not a wall: `--res`, `--round`, `--smooth`,
`--cap`, `FOE_COL`, `FOE_LIFT`. Worth pushing before committing to a whole roster.

**What it still does not solve:** animation. A voxel model gives angles, not a walk cycle. The
prize is splitting the model into limb groups and posing them before the raster, which is only
possible now that the enemy is 3D — that, not the look, is why this pipeline is worth keeping.

**Verified:** `node tools/selftest_foe.js` → 35/35 (roles are all names `rasterCar` knows, faces

## 2026-11-09 — BOOST upgrade: the recharge, not the shove

**Goal:** a fifth thing to buy at the Scrap Saints, for the boost. `BOOST_TIME` also went
**0.9 -> 1.0s** (0.9 was too short to feel). Config: `BOOST_COOL_PER_LEVEL` in **3b**.

**What it does.** `boost` is a fifth upgrade track beside armor / health / gun / missile, and it cuts
the recharge by **1s a level: 10s at level 0, 4s at level 6.**

**Why the recharge and not the strength.** The burn is 1s against a 10s recharge, so at level 0 you
hold a charge **9% of the time** — the cooldown is what limits the boost, by a factor of ten. Halving
it roughly doubles how many boosts a leg gives you, and it is only a timer, so nothing in the physics
moves. Making the boost *stronger* is the trap: `BOOST_ADD` is a target the regulator serves, not a
force, and section 2 of `config.js` already records that a boost which only raises the target spends
itself against the frame's front spring and gains no ground. You would have to raise `BOOST_ROOM`
with it, which pushes the car at the edge of the frame where `WALL_REEL` and the backstop live — the
stuck-car problem all over again. If it ever needs to feel *bigger*, buy `BOOST_TIME` instead:
distance scales linearly with it and it touches no geometry.

**The two places a per-level cooldown goes wrong,** both now covered by the selftest:

- `tryBoost` arming the raw `BOOST_COOL` instead of `boostCool()` — the upgrade would be inert.
- the HUD gauge at the `BST` bar reading `1 - boostCD/BOOST_COOL`. With a 4s countdown over a 10s
  constant the bar fills to 60% and stops, which looks like a broken gauge, not a wrong divisor.

`boostCoolAt(lv)` has a 0.5s floor so a future config edit can't hand out a boost that never ends.
`upgradeTotal()` counts the new track, so the repair price rises with it like the others.

**The store panel grew.** Seven rows: `H` 280 -> 300 and the row pitch 28 -> 26, or CONTINUE sat on
top of the confirm line. `drawOptions()` has an identical-looking panel line — it was left at 280.
The selftest asserts the last row clears the confirm line and the panel clears `VH`, so the next row
added will say so instead of being found in a screenshot.

**Verified:** `node tools/selftest_boost.js` → 30/30 (constants, `tryBoost`, the gauge, a real

## 2026-11-09 — Controller support; MOUSE or CONTROLLER in OPTIONS

**Goal:** play the whole game on a pad. The mouse stays as it is; the player picks one device and
keeps it. New file `pad.js`, knobs in `config.js` **7b (`PAD`)**, new `OPTIONS` screen off the title.

**The layout. Left side is the car, right side is the guns:**

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| left stick | steer (X, analog) + speed trim (Y) | right stick | the turret |
| d-pad | the same four, digital | | |
| L2 | drift (the handbrake) | R2 | missile |
| L1 | boost | R1 | machine gun |
| Square | mine | Cross | confirm · in and out of the car |
| Circle | back | Options | pause |

On foot: left stick walks, right stick looks, L3 runs, R1 is the pistol, Cross gets back in the car.

**Why the buttons landed there.** The two triggers are the two things you **squeeze and hold**: drift
through a corner on the left, the missile on the right. Both guns sit on the right hand, next to the aim
stick, so firing never costs you the aim. Boost is a **tap** and it is a driving input, so it joins drift
on the left shoulder. Mines moved to Square because they are the rarest thing in the game — you carry
2, 3 at most. The user asked for the guns on R1/R2 specifically, having first tried boost on R2.

**Three things worth knowing before changing it:**

- **The device choice settles the aim fight.** `updateTurret` used to take the mouse branch whenever
  `numPlayers===1 && mouseIn`, which in 1P is always — a right stick would have been overridden on
  every frame. There is no last-input-wins arbitration: `padMode()` (pad.js) is the single gate, and
  it wants *both* `inputMode==='pad'` and a pad actually connected. A saved `'pad'` with nothing
  plugged in quietly stays on the mouse, so nobody can lock themselves out of their own game.
- **The turret holds its bearing when the stick is released.** `padAimAngle()` returns `null` inside
  the deadzone and the caller leaves `c.turret` alone. A stick springs back to centre and a mouse
  does not; without this the gun snapped to straight-ahead every time you let go. And because
  `turret` is stored *relative* to the car, the gun keeps pointing where you aimed it while the car
  slides around underneath — that part was free.
- **The aim is screen-faithful, not world-faithful.** The ground is squashed by `KY` (0.62), so the
  stick is read as a direction *on screen* and converted the way the mouse already was: a 45° push
  comes out as 58.2° in world space. Push the stick at the thing you can see and the shot goes there.

**Presses go through `onPress`.** `padTick(dt)` runs once a frame at the top of `frame()` and
synthesises key codes (`'Enter'`, `'Escape'`, `MINE_KEY`, …), so the title, OPTIONS, garage, store,
setup and wreck screens needed no pad code of their own and cannot drift out of step with the
keyboard. Held controls are read where they are used instead. Edges last a whole frame, so more than
one reader can act on the same press.

**Screens answer to the pad even in MOUSE mode** — only the game itself is gated on the chosen
device. This was the other way round at first, and the self-test caught what that meant: someone who
has just plugged a pad in would have to reach for the keyboard to get to OPTIONS and turn the pad
on, which is the one thing they wanted the pad for. `padTick` gates the menu block on
`padConnected()` and everything below it on `padMode()`.

**Also changed:** analog steering is a real gain — `A`/`D` are a flat ±1, so the stick is the first
thing to give proportional cornering. `MENU_TOP` came down 0.62 → 0.56 because OPTIONS made it a
three-item menu and the `driving:` line had landed on top of the `ENTER` prompt. The hint line under
the canvas now follows the chosen device. Split-screen is untouched: in 2P the pad drives for P1 and
leaves the turret to P2's locked mouse rather than fighting over it.

**Verified:** `node tools/selftest_pad.js` — 59 checks on a fake standard-mapping pad, built on
`tools/headless.js`: deadzones and analog diagonals, trigger travel, edge-fires-once, menu repeat,
the KY aim conversion, the turret holding on release, and a full run through title → OPTIONS → a
live run → on foot → back in the car. It also pins the thing most likely to rot — that in MOUSE mode
the cursor still owns the turret and the stick does not touch it. Re-run it after touching input.
Layout checked in Chrome (`--headless=new --screenshot`); `tools/shot.js` is WSL-only and could not
be used here. `tools/headless.js` gained an `Image` stub, which the on-foot pistol's viewmodel needs
before it will draw at all — that gap predates this work.

**Not yet played on a real pad.** The button *indices* assume the W3C standard mapping, which is
what Chrome reports for DualSense/Xbox/8BitDo, but nothing here has been felt by a human. The
deadzones (`PAD.DZ_MOVE` 0.22, `PAD.DZ_AIM` 0.26) and `PAD.LOOK_RATE` 2.6 are first guesses — expect
to move them. The one to watch is `DZ_AIM`: too small and the turret twitches when the stick rests
off-centre, too large and short aim corrections do nothing.

### The hard part: the corner (he expects trouble here, and he is right)

The engine branches on **one global axis flag** — `vertical` — chosen per stage in
`applyStage()`, and dozens of functions read it: the forced scroll, `trackProject/trackPoint`,
the soft frame walls in `step()`, `spawnEnemy`/`updateSpawner` culling, `aiCharge/aiFlank/
aiPursue` (they steer on the axis and `ROAD_HW`), `drawTrack`, barrier paths and posts,
`buildConvoyRings`, `survivalTier` buildings, `inView`, `rescale()`'s `FRONT_MAX/BACK_EDGE/
VIEW_HH`, the layout (`LAYOUT`, `ZOOM2D`, `VCY_BIAS`). Flipping it mid-run flips all of them at
once - at the corner, cars and walls on the *old* leg would suddenly be computed on the new axis.

`PROJECT.md` already holds design notes for exactly this (the deferred **"L-route"**, search for
it): a one-way L wants an **open waypoint polyline** (straight, corner, straight) rather than a
parametric curve; convoy's `trackProject/trackPoint` are the straight-x special case of it; the
axonometric view is fixed-angle, so **the corner changes which screen axis the action runs
along** - check the corner *reads* on screen before building the rest; verify the path maths
against a hand-computable case in `tools/headless.js` first.

Suggested approach (decide with him, it is the big design call of the session):
- Make the road a **path**: forward distance `s` along a polyline (x-leg, a quarter-circle
  corner, −y-leg), lateral offset `lat`. `trackProject(x,y) → {s, lat}` and
  `trackPoint(s, lat) → {x, y, angle}` replace the axis branches; the scroll, spawner, AI and
  culling work in (s, lat) and never ask which way is up.
- The camera scrolls along `s`. The axonometric view stays fixed, so the frame's "forward" turns
  from screen-right to screen-up through the corner - the frame's soft walls, `VCY_BIAS` and the
  look-ahead must ease from convoy's values to ascent's across the corner.
- Keep it **shippable early**: a cheap first version can clear the field at the corner (no
  enemies spawn within the corner's span, the checkpoint banner shows) so nothing has to
  survive the axis change while the path maths is proven.
- Part 2 → part 3: the vertical leg simply becomes STREET's highway; switch off the forced scroll
  and spawner when the player crosses into the city (a gate/sign), turn on the BSP chunks and
  walking. `street` already assumes the −y highway at x≈0 - keep part 2 on that line.

## 2026-11-05 — Raycaster + ASCII performance

Why the walk view is fast, and how to measure it again. Full tables in `PROJECT.md`.

- **ASCII** (`asciiBlit`): was ~9,300 per-cell `drawImage` + two full-screen blend passes =
  29-58 ms/frame. Now built on the CPU into one ImageData (`ascMask`, `ascOut32`) + one
  `putImageData`: ~7 ms. Pixel diff vs old ≤ 2/255.
- **Walls:** `RC_DIRECT` writes walls/windows into `rcPix32` (was ~4,000 `fillRect` + colour
  strings). `RC_OCCLUDE` (`rcOcclude`) culls whole hidden buildings before ray tests (65-72%).
  Both on = **21-43% off a frame**; all variants byte-identical. He ruled out WebGL ("people will
  need a GPU").
- **Correctness checks** to rerun after raycaster changes: rcZ vs brute force over 200 random
  spots (0 bad of 64,000 columns); frame hashes across switch combinations.
- **Benchmark method:** inject a sync `<script>` into a temp copy, flush with
  `ctx.getImageData(0,0,1,1)` per iteration, write results into a `<pre>`, run Windows Chrome
  `--headless=new --dump-dom` (no virtual time), with and without `--disable-gpu`. Compare **ms
  saved**, not totals - headless baselines are bimodal (~6 vs ~15 ms for the same view).
- Gotcha: screenshots at run start can catch the car's invulnerability blink - set
  `cars[0].invuln = 0` in the shot setup.
