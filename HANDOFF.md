## 2026-11-23 — His play: the sentry freeze, and the shots now leave the model

Three things he reported after playing the turrets in ("this is spectacular"), and one standing
instruction attached to them: *"please don't forget that these changes cannot create a performance
burden on our game"*. Everything below is measured, because that sentence deserves numbers.

### 1. "as soon as I saw a sentry, the screen froze for a little bit"

He guessed it was the hatch next to the sentry. It was not: **the sentry's own sprite sheet**, and
the diagnosis is the one already written up for the turrets one entry below - the sentry had the
same disease the whole time and I did not look.

MEASURED, in `tools/headless.js` (the canvas is stubbed, so `rasterCar`'s pixel loop is the only
thing on the clock, which is exactly the thing being measured):

| | |
|---|---|
| whole sentry sheet, 6 poses x 16 yaws | **643 ms** |
| one POSE STRIP, 16 yaws | **~110 ms** |
| one CELL, one pose at one yaw | **~7 ms** |
| one cell of turret-02 (7478 faces) | ~13 ms |

The old code filled a STRIP at a time: pose 0 eagerly, on the frame the first sentry came into
sight, and then `foeWarm` fed in one more strip per frame as soon as anything walked. Six ~110 ms
frames back to back - half a second of screen at 9 fps, once per sheet, exactly as he described it,
and never again because the sheet is then full.

**The fix is one bake path for everything.** `foeSheetEnsure(sp, p)` (a strip) is gone and
`foeCellEnsure(sp, p, fr)` (one cell) replaces it; the turret's bespoke `turretYawEnsure` +
`sp.yaws` + `turretScratch` are gone too, folded into it, because a turret's sheet is one pose and
so for it a cell IS a yaw. One ledger, `sp.cell`, a `Uint8Array(POSES*YAWS)`, plus `sp.left` so a
full sheet costs the fillers one compare a frame instead of a scan. `sp.pm[p]` caches `posedMesh`
per pose, which used to be free (once per strip) and would otherwise be rebuilt for all 16 yaws.

Nothing is baked at sheet creation any more. The draw path pulls the one cell it is about to draw;
`foeWarm`/`turretWarm` fill the rest in the background. The POSE POLICY is unchanged and still
deliberate: pose 0 always, the five walk poses only while something is walking, so a sentry that
never takes a step never pays for the stride.

`FOE_RIG.WARM` is **1**, and that is a measured choice, not a default. Over 240 real on-foot frames
with a sentry walking at the camera (`drawWalkView()`, harness baseline 12.7 ms/frame):

| WARM | worst frame | frames over 25 ms |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 38 ms | **7 / 240** |
| 2 | 44 ms | 32 / 240 |
| 3 | 46 ms | 31 / 240 |

Four times fewer long frames at 1, and the ceiling is the same either way (one background cell plus
at most one the draw path pulls on demand). Filling slower costs nothing visible, because a cell
missed is one 7 ms frame and not a stall. **Do not raise it to fill the sheet sooner.**

Steady state, in the maze with 11 turrets up and the sentries walking: `foeWarm` and `turretWarm`
cost **0.00 ms**, `updateFoes` settles at **0.23 ms**, and `rasterCar` is called **0 times** in the
last 10 frames of a 180-frame run. That is the answer to his instruction.

### 2. The bolt left from above the barrel

|---|---|---|---|

| turret-02 missiles | `tail` | two clusters: x 12.3..13.4, \|y\| 10.5..14.2, z 14.8..19.1 | `fwd 12.8, side 12.2, z 16.7` |

### 3. The missiles come off the arms

*"the rockets should leave its arms"* - they do, and it was not hard, because the carve says where
the arms are. `side` is a MAGNITUDE and `f.side` picks the arm, flipped at the START of the windup
so the smoke pours out of the arm the missile then leaves. Measured: consecutive rounds are born
**24.4 units apart**, which is 2 x 12.2, and 17.7 units out from the centre, which is
`hypot(12.8, 12.2)`. The reference render he sent shows exactly this - two outboard pods, nothing on
the centreline.

Pre-existing and NOT touched: a missile in flight draws as a flat amber `fillRect` at r 8, which is
a big square at close range. His note was about where they come from.

### 4. "it starts shooting with the left arms but shoots with the right"

He reported this after the arms went in, having first seen it stuck on one arm. Both symptoms, one
cause, and it is a **name collision I created**: the arm lived in `f.side`, and `side` already
belongs to the sentry - it means "lean off to ONE side, you are wedged on a corner", -1/0/+1,
written by `foeThink` and by `foeStuck`. **`foeStuck` loops over every foe with no turret filter.**

So, for a turret, every frame:

* **calm** (`mad` false, which is a turret's normal state - nothing sets it): the first branch fires
  and `f.side = 0`. The windup latches arm 1, `foeStuck` zeroes it before the next frame, and every
  tell and the shot come off arm 0. *Always the same arm.*
* **angry** (it has taken a round): the second branch fires, it has not moved - it cannot move - so
  `f.side = f.side || (Math.random() < 0.5 ? -1 : 1)` gives it a random sign that then STICKS,
  because of the `||`. Both -1 and +1 are truthy, so both pick the SAME arm. *Always the other arm.*
* **turning angry mid-windup**: the branch changes between the first puff of smoke and the shot, so
  the tell leaves one arm and the missile leaves the other. *Exactly what he saw.*

Fixed three ways, because one is not enough: the arm is now `f.arm`, its own field that nothing else
writes; it is LATCHED when the windup opens, because the tell and the shot are computed up to 1.2 s
apart; and `foeStuck` returns early on a turret, which is true anyway - a thing bolted to the floor
cannot be wedged on a corner.

**It alternates, strictly - it is not a coin flip.** He asked for a coin flip and then withdrew it;
alternation is the better answer for a rack cycling its tubes, and a flip would fire the same arm
twice now and again for no reason visible on the model. `f.arm` starts at 0 and the latch happens
before the first shot, so the first missile of a turret's life leaves the `+M.side` arm.

The lesson, which is the reusable part: `spawnTurret` builds a record that is deliberately
foe-shaped so it can reuse shot collision, blast splash, `foeDamage` and the billboard list unchanged
(that is the whole design, see the entry below) - and the price is that EVERY generic loop over
`foes` runs on turrets too. `foeSeparate` and `foeBlock` were given turret filters when the turrets
went in; `foeStuck` was missed. Before adding a field to that record, grep for who else writes it.

### The tests that now hold this down (`selftest_foe` 471)

* `the sentry sheet is filled a cell at a time, not a strip at a time` - 9 checks, including that a
  fresh sheet has NOTHING baked, that one cell is one cell and not its row, that `posedMesh` is
  cached per pose, and the pose policy both ways round.
* `the shot leaves the part of the model you can see` - 13 checks that read the SHIPPED CARVE, group
  its faces by role, and assert the muzzle is inside the measured tip. Re-carve at another
  resolution, or move which part is `lamp`, and these fail instead of the muzzle quietly drifting.
* `the missile rack and its two arms` - 5 checks, and they were VERIFIED AGAINST THE BUG: with the
  old code put back, 4 of the 5 fail. It runs a whole windup frame by frame with `foeStuck` and
  `foeSeparate` running alongside and the turret taking a round halfway through, then asserts the
  smoke is UNANIMOUS - every puff off the arm the missile then leaves - and that consecutive shots
  come off opposite arms. The first version of this test averaged the smoke's side and passed with
  the bug in, because the burst after the turret turns angry outnumbers the burst before it. An
  average hides a tell that starts on one arm and ends on the other, which was the entire report.

### The trap I fell into, worth not repeating

I measured `rcSprites()` by calling it directly and got 220 ms a frame with `rasterCar` running 23
times per frame forever. That was my harness: **`rcSprites(camX, camY, bearing)` takes the camera**,
I passed none, every billboard got a NaN yaw, and `sp.cell[NaN] = 1` is silently swallowed by a
typed array - so the ledger never recorded anything and every sprite re-baked. Driven properly
through `drawWalkView()` it is 0 bakes a frame. `foeCellEnsure` now guards its index, because that
failure is invisible: no error, no wrong pixel, just 200 ms a frame.

### The trap doors

`F.hatch` is a `Uint8Array` beside `F.foes` and `F.meds` - 0 none, 1 shut, 2 open - and `F.turrets`
holds a complete turret per lid from the moment the floor is generated, `hidden`. **A turret exists
before it can be seen**, which is the point: the lid he walks over on the way down is the same
object that comes up behind him on the way back.

They are DRAWN BY SAMPLING THE FLOOR, not as billboards. `dcBackground` now takes the camera and,
for the rows whose `perp` is inside `DC.HATCH_SEE` (460, inside `DC.FOG` 560), samples every other
column into world space exactly as the street's floor does and asks `dcHatchAt`. One cell lookup
and one distance per sample, so a lid is a true circle on the floor that grows as you walk up to it -
rim, seam and all - instead of a sprite pretending to lie down. The lid colours stay in
`FLOOR_RGB`'s own cool hue and are only lifted, and they heat with the floor, or they would be cold
grey discs in a room that has gone red.

**Three places build a floor record and all three now need the two new fields** - `dcFloorState`,
`dcLabReroll` and (reading them) `dcPopulate`. The lab re-roll was the one that broke: its own
comment already said "a lab floor that is missing half the fields is a lab that lies about the
game", and `dcPopulate` now READS `F.turrets` to keep the sentries off the lids.

### The mix: an exact share, decided per CELL

Two things went wrong here and both are worth keeping.

1. `rng.next() < TURRET_MIX` once per turret is **binomial**, and at n = 11 that is far too wide for
   a dial he will turn: the first floor of the shipped seed came out **nine missile racks out of
   eleven** (a 0.4% draw) and that is the floor he learns the mechanic on.
2. Counting them exactly and then shuffling with Fisher-Yates off `rng` came out **sorted**, because
   the stream at that point returns a run of small values and a small `r` makes every swap pick an
   index near nought.

**`cityRng` is NOT broken** - measured at 0.4495 of a million draws below 0.45, deciles flat to
±0.8%. It was a short low run, which a good generator is allowed to do and which a shuffle must not
depend on not happening. The fix is to hash each CELL and take the lowest `want` of them: exact
count, deterministic per floor, independent of both the stream position and the order `dcPlaces`
returned the spots in. Now 5 racks on every floor, closest two 4-9 cells apart.

### THE BAKE WAS NOT FREE - read this before adding another model

He asked, reasonably: *"since everything is prerendered and the sprites are saved for later, use the
options with more faces, that won't give us performance issues, right?"*

**That is true of the drawing and false of the bake.** Measured: 32 yaws of `turret2@64` (7,478
faces) in one go is **~400 ms**. A quarter-second freeze - triggered on the first frame a missile
rack comes into view, which is a floor that has just opened eleven hatches at once. `turret1@64` is
~100 ms, and the sentry's first pose is ~106 ms, which is the existing baseline and the reason
`foeSheetEnsure` already fills **one walk pose at a time**.

A turret has one row, so it is filled a few YAWS at a time instead:

- `bakeTurretSheet` now creates the sheet and bakes **nothing**; `sp.yaws` is the per-yaw ledger and
  `sp.done[0]` is set purely so the sentry's row filler can never touch the sheet.
- `turretYawEnsure(sp, fr)` bakes one yaw (~3 ms turret-01, ~13 ms turret-02). `drawTurretBillboard`
  asks for the one it needs, so a turret that becomes visible early is still correct.
- `turretWarm()` runs beside `foeWarm()` in `updateFoes` and fills `TURRET.WARM` (1) yaws of ONE
  sheet a frame.
- **`dcRelease` starts both sheets**, so the 1.2 s rise is the cover they fill under: 64 yaws
  between them at one a frame is 1.07 s, and it fits.

Result: worst single frame **40 ms instead of a 400 ms freeze**, both sheets full inside the rise.
The `@64` carves are kept, as he asked. *(Numbers are from the headless harness, whose canvas is
stubbed - the ratios are what matter, not the absolute milliseconds.)*

### The rise, drawn by clipping

There is no second model and no animation. The sprite is **clipped to the part of it that has
cleared the floor**: `sp.ay` is the sheet row the model's z = 0 sits on, so the floor line is
`(1 - rise)*h*vsc` rows above it and everything below that row is still underground. What comes up
first is whatever is at the top of the model - the barrel on one, the missile arms on the other.
The full frame is drawn once it is up rather than using the clipped path at `rise` 1, because the
frame's bottom rows hold the base's near face (`KY_FPS` tilts it into view).

Three paths gate on `rise < 1` - the bolt collision, the pistol and `foeBlock` - or a turret you can
kill before it clears the lid makes the whole rise cosmetic.

## 2026-11-23 — Two turrets through the pipeline: an OBJ reader and an FBX reader

His ask: *"I just added 2 new folders with 2 different turret assets... unfortunately from different
authors, so they have different stacks. Lets process these 2 turrets through our voxelization +
sprite extraction pipeline and add them to the voxcar-viewer.html file so I can test it out"*

**Both are in the viewer** (`turret1@48/@64`, `turret2@48/@64`, labelled `(turret)`), rendered
through the game's own bakes. Nothing is wired into the game yet - that is the turret ENTITY, and
it is step 7's job.

### The two "stacks", and what each one needed

| | turret-01 | turret-02 |
|---|---|---|
| ships | `.obj` + `.fbx` + 157 MB `.blend`, textures in a `.zip` | **FBX only**, three `.rar` files |
| parts | 3 named objects, ONE material | 7 objects, 6 materials, mirrored halves as separate instances |
| tris | 9,917 (after fanning quads) | 341,542 |
| axes | Y up, barrel +Z | Y up, +Z - but every node PRE-rotated -90 X (see below) |

Neither could go through the pipeline as it stood:

- **`tools/read_obj.py` (new).** `trimesh.load(force='mesh')` MERGES every object into one soup, and
  for a turret the three objects - base / body / barrel - are exactly what we want coloured
  differently. Numpy-only, same return shape as `read_3ds.py`.
- **`tools/read_fbx.py` (new).** trimesh cannot read FBX at all (it needs Autodesk's closed SDK),
  and blender/assimp are not installed here. Binary FBX 7.5: node records, zlib'd typed arrays,
  polygons delimited by a negated last index, and geometry wired to its Model (and so to its NAME
  and its placement) through `Connections`, with Models nesting - so the transform chain is walked
  rather than trusted. This is the reader that lets the pipeline take the whole FBX-only class of
  download, which is most asset packs. **Two traps, and both of them bit:**
  - **Version >= 7500 widens three header fields to 64 bits.** Read as 32-bit, every offset past
    the first node is nonsense. Caught before the first build.
  - **A node is NOT translate-rotate-scale.** `_matrix` read only `Lcl Translation/Rotation/Scaling`
    and that shipped a visibly broken turret-02 (see below). The real chain is
    `T * Roff * Rp * Rpre * R * Rpost^-1 * Rp^-1 * Soff * Sp * S * Sp^-1`, and the factor that
    matters is **`PreRotation`**. Max and Maya exports hang the Z-up -> Y-up conversion off it:
    `GlobalSettings` says `UpAxis: Y` with `OriginalUpAxis: Z`, the mesh data is still Z-up, the
    `Lcl Translation` beside it is already Y-up, and PreRotation is the ONLY thing reconciling the
    two. Also honour `RotationActive` (pre/post rotation count only when it is set) and
    `RotationOrder` (six Euler orders; the default XYZ composes `Rz @ Ry @ Rx`).

#### turret-02 came out broken, and what it looked like (2026-11-23, his catch)

He put the vendor's render beside the viewer: *"this one is completely broken."* It was. Every one
of the 7 objects was present and both mirrored instances were placed, so nothing was being DROPPED -
each part was individually 90 degrees out of its own placement, which is the specific signature of
the dropped `PreRotation`. The base ring stood on edge (79.6 x 78.4 across, 18.7 thick, lying in XY
while the body stacked up +y) and the rusty gimbal had flown off into a corner on its own.

**How it was found, and the lesson:** bounding boxes could not settle it - a part 79.6 x 78.4 x 18.7
is a plausible base pad under either axis convention, which is the same ambiguity that made the
up-axis unreadable in the first place. What settled it in one look was dumping an **ASCII
silhouette of the assembled model down each axis with one letter per part** (base B, gimbal M, body
O, arm A, missile m). The gimbal sitting alone in a corner of the `looking down x` panel is
unmistakable, and no amount of staring at numbers had shown it. Reach for that first next time a
multi-part import looks wrong; the script pattern is a dozen lines (triangle-sampled points, not
vertices - vertex clouds are far too sparse to read).

After the fix the model sits on `y = 0` and the stack reads base -> gimbal -> body -> arms ->
missiles, matching the vendor render, and **`nose: "+z"` was right all along** - the missiles come
out at +z. That was the open question from the first pass; it is now answered for turret-02.

**A canary worth adding, not yet added:** a misassembled import leaves the voxel model in two or
more DISCONNECTED blobs, and `voxelize` already runs a flood fill for `outside`. Counting solid
components and warning when there is more than one would have caught this before the screenshot.
- **`trimesh` is now imported WHERE IT IS USED**, not at module scope. A turret recipe that touches
  no trimesh code was still dying on `import trimesh` in an interpreter that had none (see the
  environment note below for which one to run).
- **`--res` now counts voxels along the model's LONGEST axis, not the nose.** For a car those are
  the same thing. For turret-02, a squat tower whose nose axis is its SHORTEST, counting 48 along
  the nose gave a 51x85x109 grid and a **400 KB** file; by its height it is **90 KB**.
  **Proven a no-op for the cars by rebuilding them**, not by argument: all nine `assets/voxcars/*.js`
  and both turrets come back byte-for-byte identical. Getting that exact needed one more line -
  the axis span is taken from the recipe's DECLARED `length`/`height` when that axis is the longest,
  and only measured when it is not. A measured extent agrees with the declared one to within an ULP,
  and an ULP is enough to flip a rounding tie in `lo`, which rewrites a file for no reason.
- **`height` as an alternative to `length` in a recipe.** A car is a length - you say how long the
  car is. A turret is a height: you say how tall the thing in the corridor is, and the barrel is
  however long the model says it is.
- **Recipes take `objects` (name -> role, PREFIX matched) and `materials` (name -> role).** Prefix,
  because an exporter numbers its instances: turret-02's launcher arms arrive as `turret left arm`
  and `turret left arm001` and both want the same role.

### THE UP-AXIS WAS MEASURED, NOT ASSUMED

Bounding boxes are ambiguous - turret-02's base is 80 x 78 x 19, which reads as a base pad under
either Y-up or Z-up. So: dense silhouettes per axis first (triangle-sampled, not vertex clouds -
vertices are far too sparse to read), then **both** variants were built and rendered. Z-up lays the
turret on its back, the base disc standing up as a vertical wall. **Y-up, nose +Z** for both.

**Which END of the nose axis is forward** - `+z` vs `-z`, front vs back, which decides where a
turret SHOOTS - was the open question from the first pass. **Answered: `+z` for both.** Once
turret-02 was assembled correctly its missiles plainly come out at +z, and turret-01's barrel was
never in doubt.

### Sizes, and what the viewer needed

`height` 18 for turret-01 (45 long on the barrel axis - it is a long gun) and 26 for turret-02
(26 tall on a decorative ring 35 ACROSS - once assembled properly, its widest axis is the base, not
its height). A walker's eye is `WALK_EYE` 16 and a sentry is ~22 tall, so both read as emplacements
a person stands next to.

| | @48 | @64 |
|---|---|---|
| turret1 | 35 KB, 1,066 faces, grid 51x27x23 | 52 KB, 1,541 faces, grid 67x35x30 |
| turret2 | 153 KB, 4,479 faces, grid 51x51x40 | 262 KB, 7,478 faces, grid 67x67x52 |

**turret-02 is the expensive one and the ring is why.** Its concentric ornamental base merges
badly under greedy faces - a disc of rings is close to the worst case - and it is pure decoration
at `S = 1.6 px/unit`. @48 (153 KB) is the cut to use in game; if that is still too much, the base
object is one line in the recipe to drop and the turret above it is the half you can see.

**turret-01's muzzle is `lamp`, not `dark`** (his note: *"it is black, so it is very hard to see
in our first person rendering"*). `dark` is #241d2c against an already dark corridor, which hides
the ONE thing a player must be able to read off a turret - where it is pointing. `lamp` (#ffe7a0)
is the only role that is a fixed colour rather than a shade of the body, so it stays bright
whatever colour the thing is tinted. `accent` (body x1.32) and `metal` (#585265) are the
alternatives, one word each.

The viewer grew **two flags where it had one**. They used to be the same thing because the sentry
was the only non-car: `FIGURE` now means "posed by `FOE_RIG`" and `STOOD` means "framed standing on
the floor and lit with the brightness lift". **A turret is STOOD but not a FIGURE** - it stands on
a base like a figure stands on its feet and it shows the camera the same badly-lit +y faces, but it
has no legs to swing and it traverses smoothly, so it keeps the cars' 32 yaws rather than the
sentry's 16.

## 2026-11-17 — Quest 2 — THE DATACENTER: the worm, three floors, and the heat

**Plan, not built.** His design, decided over one session. This **supersedes the Quest 2 sketch**
further down this file ("a fight in a huge city square"), and the differences are deliberate -
read "What this overturns" before building anything from the old entry.

### The shape

1. **The Saints' shop** (built, 2026-11-12) hands over **the worm**.
2. **The square** - the datacenter stands on it, as the shipped radio already promises. You go in.
3. **Three floors down**, on foot, first person. Labyrinth.
4. **The central unit, floor 3.** Root access to the whole plant. You plant the worm there.
5. **The worm takes everything** and reverses the cooling. The building starts to cook.
6. **You climb out** through the heat, by service stairs or by the elevator.

### 1. What the Saints give you: ONE thing, and it is hardware

**The worm.** Not a bomb, not software - a **physical module** the Saints salvaged, that he has to
fit by hand. Two reasons, both load-bearing:

- The canon rule *"He is a mechanic... use that instead of inventing specialists"* (this file,
  "Quest design"). A hacker payload makes him somebody else. Fitting a salvaged part into a machine
  that does not want it is the most on-brand sentence in the game.
- **The engine can only render places and verbs.** The pour proved the shape: go to a place, hold
  still, hold a key for N seconds, world reacts. Software is a terminal and a progress bar;
  hardware is an open panel and a part going in - same timer, but one of them exists in a
  raycaster. Malware that arrives as a part is still malware; it just has a silhouette.

It is **one item**, bought at the shop that already exists. The three-part assembly (fuel /
oxidiser / detonator) from the old sketch is **dropped** - the Saints are a body shop, they stock
parts, and one payload is a fraction of the build.

### 2. The plant IS the boss fight

Three floors of labyrinth teach exactly one verb: *move, avoid, do not get cornered.* The core room
takes it away. Planting is **the pour's verb** - hold position, hold the key, N seconds, cannot
move - and every guard on the floor converges on you.

That is the set-piece, it is the one thing a maze can never ask of you, and it reuses a mechanic
that already ships and is already tested. **No boss enemy has to be built for this quest.**

### 3. The heat is the timer, and there is no clock

The worm reverses the HVAC to **50 °C** (that is 122 °F, not 100 - the figure matters if it ever
reaches a HUD or a radio line; real hardware throttles about 35-40 °C and starts dying past 45-50).

Why this is the whole design and not set dressing:

- **He is a robot.** Datacenters are cooled because machines cannot take heat. The weapon that
  kills the building is lethal to *him* by the identical mechanism. The servers dying and his hull
  dying are one system.
- **Heat rises, and he is climbing.** The core is at the bottom and the exit is at the top, so the
  way out runs *into* the heat. Pressure increases as he approaches safety, for free, out of
  physics. Descent is a gradient down into cold; escape is a gradient up into fire.
- **No countdown number anywhere.** The timer is a temperature.

Mechanics:

| | |
|---|---|
| Above a threshold | heat **eats hull** - it does not instant-fail. The HULL track is escape insurance, and skipping it is felt. |
| The repair rig | **does not work in the heat.** It already needs him still and unhit; standing still in a cooking building should be death. Kills the heal-through exploit with no special case. |
| Cold aisles | **CUT - his call.** Saved for a later datacenter. |

**Consequence of cutting the cold aisles, flagged on purpose:** the ramp is now one-way with no
recovery, and since standing still is what refills the sprint tank (`WALK_STAM_STILL`, 2x) and
standing still is now lethal, the climb is **mostly walking, sprint saved for emergencies.** At a
3 s base tank that is genuinely tense and probably right - but it is the number most likely to need
tuning, and the design has no release valve left to soften it with. **Build the heat curve as one
adjustable constant from day one.**

### The maze code: where it is and the one thing wrong with it

`/home/cmoryah/proj/maze-explorer3/` - and `src/campaignMaze.js` (895 lines) is already the
BSP-plus-labyrinth hybrid he described wanting: `BSPNode3D` splits the floor into regions,
`generateMiniMaze` fills each leaf with a recursive-backtracker maze, `createCorridorBetween` joins
them. Right structure, already written.

**THE FLAW - fix this first.** `createCorridors` walks the BSP tree and calls `connectRegions`
**once per node**, joining two subtrees at their nearest pair of leaves. Each leaf interior is a
*perfect* maze. The whole floor is therefore a **spanning tree: exactly one path between any two
cells, zero cycles.**

Tolerable for the descent (exploring a perfect maze is merely slow). **Fatal for the escape:**

- You can never loop around a pursuer - every encounter is a corridor standoff you must win.
- Every wrong turn is a *full backtrack*, while the building cooks and the spawns pile up.
- Backtracking cost grows faster than area, and floor 3 is the biggest.

**A chase needs cycles.** Fix: **braid** the maze - after generation, knock out a wall on a
fraction of the dead ends (~30-50 %) - plus give some BSP joins a *second* corridor. Decides
whether the escape is tense or infuriating.

### Decoupling the two knobs (his call)

maze-explorer3 ties size and room count to one level number:
`baseSize = 15 * 1.25^(level-1)`, `numSplits = level - 1`. So level 1 is 15x15 with **zero** BSP
splits - a pure maze, no rooms. That runs **backwards** from the fiction: floor 1 is the offices,
the floor that most wants rooms; floor 3 is the big maze, which wants the fewest. **Size and splits
become separate per-floor numbers in `config.js`.**

Floor 3 stays the biggest - his call - but only somewhat, not hugely.

### Our raycaster, not theirs (his call)

Do **not** port `maze-explorer3/src/raycaster.js`. Twisted-racing's is better and already carries
the fog, the LOD, ASCII mode, billboards and the gun. The port is: take the maze **grid**, convert
wall runs into ring records shaped like `buildingRings` output (`{pts, h, col, len, fs}`), feed the
existing renderer. **Merge collinear walls into long segments** rather than one ring per cell, or
the raycaster gets handed thousands of tiny rings. `teleportCells` is already in that file and is
the obvious hook for the stairs between floors.

Note the interior is **first-person only**: the axonometric camera culls every face whose normal is
not screen-down (proved the same day by the Saints' door, which never draws up there). That is a
mode switch the game has not made before, and it is probably good - a raycaster is a corridor
renderer and this is what it is best at.

### His third play (2026-11-18): "floor 3 is only 2 regions, I want 3 or 4"

Fair, and it was not a tuning problem - **the knob could not express it.** `splits` was a count of

**`splits` is now `regions`, an EXACT count.** maze.js cuts the BIGGEST leaf one at a time until

are exact on every floor; 19x19 caps at 4, 23x23 reaches 5-6 some seeds, 27x27 is reliable to 5

### Step 6 built (2026-11-18): the heat - and the number that reshaped it

`dcTemp(floor)` is the whole timer. **No countdown anywhere.** Floor 0 is the top and cooks first;
each floor below starts `HEAT_LAG` seconds later, so the way out is always hotter than the way in

red toward `HEAT_TINT` as it cooks, so the temperature is readable with the HUD off.

cannot carry a steep ramp: anything that bites integrates into hundreds of hull. The first numbers

|---|---|---|---|
| optimal | 36 | lives | comfortable |
| 1.3x | 74 | dies | needs a med-kit |
| 1.6x | 120 | dies | dies |
| 2.0x | 201 | dies | dies |

`w.pitch` is never read. That is an **infinite vertical cylinder** of radius `FOE.HIT_R` round the
line you face: aim at your own boots, kill a sentry 500 units away. There was no z in the test at
all, so there was no way for it to miss high or low.

**What it is now.** The walk view is a **shift, not a tilt** — `drawWalkView` slides the horizon by
`RC_VD*tan(pitch)` and every column stays a world-vertical plane — so a point at height z and
perpendicular distance d draws at `y = horizon + (eye - z)*RC_VD/d`. Inverting that at the
crosshair (`RC_H/2`) gives the ray the round actually flies down:

```
z(d) = eye - DROP*d,      DROP = (RC_H/2 - horizon)/RC_VD
```

`gunFire` walks that ray. A target is a **box**: the lateral radius it always had, and `z` inside
`[0, H]` — `FOE.H` for a sentry, the new `PISTOL.CAR_H` for a car. **The lateral radii are
untouched**, so none of the existing car/sentry tuning moves. And `DROP > 0` now clamps the range
at `eye/DROP`: aiming down, the round ploughs into the road and nothing past that point is a
target.

### The number to know before you play it: ~210 units

`rcInit` puts the resting horizon at **0.44** of the pane ("more road, less empty sky") and the
crosshair is at **0.5**. So the line of fire is already a few degrees down at pitch 0, and at
`WALK_EYE` 16 with `RC_VD` 146.6 it **meets the road at about 213 units**. Past that you have to
tilt up and put the crosshair ON him.

That is not a bug introduced here — it is what the picture has always shown, and you can read it
straight off `.shots/aim-level-hit.png`: level, at 150 units, the crosshair sits on the sentry's
shins. It is the hit test that used to disagree with the picture. But it *is* a change in how the
gun feels at range, and **if it plays badly the fix is a design choice, not a bug fix**: either
leave it (the shot goes where the crosshair is, which is what was asked for), or move the
crosshair up onto the horizon so the gun fires level. Do not "fix" it by putting the pitch back
out of the test.

`PISTOL.AIM_PAD` (2) is the forgiveness: **raycaster pixels**, turned into world units at the
target's distance, so it is a constant slack on screen rather than in the world. An ASCII cell is
`ASC_CH` = 3 of those tall, so the rule is "within two thirds of a character of his silhouette".
Set it to 0 for an exact box.

### What this did NOT fix

- **The lateral is still a world cylinder**, `FOE.HIT_R` 8 against a body radius (`FOE.R`) of 6.
  Deliberate: making it angular like the vertical would move the car numbers too.
- **The wall clamp ignores pitch.** `bd` is capped by `rcZ[RC_W >> 1]` whatever your elevation, so
  you cannot shoot over a wall the ray clears. It almost never shows, because the kerb is a `low`
  ring and out of `rcZ` already and everything else in there is tall. The fix, if it ever matters,
  is an `rcZH[x]` beside `rcZ[x]` holding that wall's height.
- **The car's cannon and missile are still flat 2D** — `index.html` ~2176 is
  `Math.hypot(f.x - s.x, f.y - s.y) < FOE.SHOT_R`, and a shot's `z` is a *render* value only. That
  is the whole car-combat layer, and moving it would move the balance of every weapon.
- **A sentry is one box, with no head.** Now that the ray has a z, a headshot band is a few lines:
  test `z` against the top of `FOE.H` for a multiplier.

**It costs nothing.** `gunFire` is per SHOT, not per frame, and `PISTOL.RATE` caps it at 3.3 a
second. Measured in Chrome with 50 sentries in the list: **0.37 us a shot**, the added work being
one `Math.tan` and a compare per candidate. There is no per-frame cost at all.

**Checked.** `tools/selftest_foe.js` is **171** (was 162): level-at-120 hits, aimed-at-the-road
misses *however well lined up*, over-the-head misses, the full look-down lands the round under 40
units away, and the drop the test uses is asserted to be the renderer's own geometry rather than a
second copy of it. Screenshots `.shots/aim-level-hit.png` (crosshair on his shins -> hit, HP 999
-> 981) and `.shots/aim-down-miss.png` (crosshair on bare road, sentry near the horizon -> miss,
impact at 36 units). `scrapheap-gun-lab.html` regenerated.

## 2026-11-10 — The kerb hides what stands behind it

His report: *"in the city main road, in ASCII, there is this drawn curb which is basically a
plane. It is drawn behind the characters - even if an enemy is behind it, the enemy still appears
in front of it."* Right, and it was never going to do anything else.

**What the kerb is.** On foot the road barrier is drawn at `WALK_BARRIER_H` (7) instead of
`BARRIER_H` (16) — you step over it, so at the gunner's height it read as a wall. 7 is under
`WALK_EYE` (16), so `rcWalls` also draws its **top ribbon**, and that is the "plane" he is looking
at. `buildCityRings` / `buildConvoyRings` flag that ring **`low: onFoot`**.

**Why it hid nothing.** `rcZ` is **one depth per column**, and `rcWalls` fills it from the nearest
hit *that is not `low`*. `rcSprites` then culls with a single test, `perp > rcZ[col]`. So a `low`
ring is invisible to the only occlusion a billboard gets.

That was deliberate, and the reason is still good: one depth per column can only hide a billboard
**whole**. The kerb is 7 units tall and a sentry (`FOE.H`) is 22, so culling one standing behind it
would be a worse bug than drawing its boots over the kerb. The old comment says as much — *"a
`low` ring does not hide billboards, so the parked car still shows over it"*. The mistake was
treating "hide all of it" and "hide none of it" as the only two options.

**The fix: clip, don't cull.** `rcWalls` now records the front of the nearest low wall per column —
`rcLowD[x]` its perpendicular depth, `rcLowY[x]` the **topmost row** it covers (the top ribbon's
far edge, `yFar`, not the face's `top`, because the ribbon reaches higher). `rcSprites` calls
`rcLowClip(perp, sx, rad)` before each billboard: in the columns where the kerb is nearer, the
canvas clip is cut at that row; everything else is left whole, and the clip is popped after the
sprite. `rcZ` and the tall-wall cull are untouched.

### The clip is ONE integer rectangle, and that is the whole cost of the feature

The first cut did the honest thing: a clip path with one rectangle per run of equal cut rows,
1-4 per sprite. It worked, and it cost **+345 us a frame** - `rcSprites` went 0.114 -> 0.467 ms,
and the pixel walk frame went up **50%**. Benchmarked in Chrome (no virtual time, see PROJECT.md)
on the 320x180 buffer, 20 occluded sprites a frame, us for all 20:

| what the drawImage goes through | us |
|---|---|
| no clip | 179 |
| **1 rect, integer rows** | **209** |
| 1 rect, fractional rows | 272 |
| 2 rects | 505 |
| 4 rects | 699 |

`clip()` itself is nearly free (~0.8 us); **drawing through it** is what costs. A single
axis-aligned rectangle on integer rows keeps Skia's fast blit, and a second rectangle - or a
fractional edge - drops the blit onto a path that costs 4x. So `rcLowClip` takes the **lowest row
the kerb reaches across the sprite** and cuts flat there, in one rect.

What that gives up: the cut no longer follows the kerb's slope. Measured on a 49-billboard frame
the kerb's top moves **1-4 rows** across a sprite's span, an ASCII cell is `ASC_CH` = 3 rows tall,
and taking the LOWEST row errs toward showing more of the sprite - so the error is a couple of
boot pixels over the kerb, never a gap above it. The two versions are indistinguishable in a
screenshot.

**What it costs now**, same scene (49 billboards, 20 of them clipped - heavier than the game puts
on screen):

| | clip off | clip on | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rcSprites` | 0.114 ms | 0.132 ms | **+19 us** |
| walk frame, pixel | 0.671 ms | 0.676 ms | +5 us (**0.75%**) |
| walk frame, ASCII | 3.841 ms | 3.847 ms | +6 us (**0.15%**) |

Both frame figures are at the noise floor. `rcWalls` also fills 320 floats a frame it did not
before; it did not move off 0.18 ms.

Two more details that keep it safe:

- **`rcLowY` is stored as a whole row.** That is what makes the rect integer, which is half the
  table above.
- **Driving costs nothing.** `low` is `onFoot` at build time, so at the wheel there are no low
  rings, `rcLowAny` stays false and `rcLowClip` returns on its first line. Probed in Chrome:
  `{lowRings: 0, rcLowAny: false}`. `rad` is a generous guess (`90*scale` for a car, `50*scale`
  otherwise) and is only used to pick the row, so getting it small costs accuracy, never a
  sliced sprite.

**Checked.** `tools/selftest_foe.js` is **162** (was 150) — a new head records the kerb's depth and
row, asserts a sprite behind it clips and one in front does not, and asserts the sentry behind it
is still in `gatherBillboards` (clipped, *not* culled). Screenshots: pixel mode, one sentry each
side of the kerb — the far one's boots stop at the kerb's top edge, the near one draws over the
band intact (`.shots/kerb-before.png`, `.shots/kerb-after.png`). `scrapheap-gun-lab.html` regenerated (`python3 tools/make_gun_lab.py`).

**The trap for next time:** the canvas in `tools/headless.js` stubs `clip`/`save`/`restore`, so the
suite can only prove the mechanism — which columns clip, and at what row. Whether the cut lands on
the right pixel is a `tools/shot.js` question, and the honest shot is pixel mode, not ASCII: ASCII
averages a 22-unit sentry into a few glyphs and will hide a wrong answer from you.

## 2026-11-10 — Moving sentries: a second population driven by the clock

His ask: keep the standing sentries exactly as they are, add **movers** that walk A to B, make it
**very lightweight**, parametrize everything, and *"if the thing is not on screen, why bother"*.

`FOE_WALK` (config.js **3f**) is a completely separate sample. Nothing in it reads `FOE_POP`, and
`FOE_WALK.ON = false` removes every mover without touching the standing city.

### The one idea the whole thing rests on

**A mover on duty is never simulated. Its position is read out of the clock.**

```
route        = an arc of its block's pavement ring (two distances along it)
position(t)  = ringPoint(ring, lerp(s0, s1, pingpong(t)))
```

That buys four things at once:

- **No pathfinding.** The ring is the same line `foeSpot` already stands sentries on, so it is
  pavement by construction. `FLOW` is no use here anyway — it points at the player and its
  clearances are a *car's*.
- **Nothing to run off screen.** A mover with no body is one plan entry. We do not step it, we
  *evaluate* it when it comes into range. Measured: **1,298 routes in memory, 161 with bodies.**
- **No teleport on return.** Walk away, come back, and the schedule says where it should be at this
  instant whether or not anyone was watching. Verified: **0.000 units** off.
- **No drift, no collision, no stuck.** None of `foeCollide` / `foeSeparate` / `foeStuck` runs for a
  mover on duty.

The only stateful part is being **shot**: a provoked mover leaves the schedule, chases on the normal
`FOE_AI` path, then rejoins.

### Routes

A route is an arc of the block's pavement ring, inset by `LANE` (0.25 of `CITY_STYLE.PAVE`) so the
movers walk the **kerb** side and the standers keep the middle.

- **POLICE has `loop: true`** — a full lap of the ring, one direction, no turning round, no pause.
  **1166 to 2894 units, median 1912.** It is the longest route in the game *and* the cheapest,
  because there is no ping-pong to work out.
- **The other four ping-pong.** After he asked for 4x longer: **min 900, median 1490, max 2397.**

**The ceiling is geometric, and he should know it:** a ping-pong route lives on ONE block's ring, so
it cannot be longer than that ring (1166 min, 1904 median). 4x the old median (521 → 2084) is longer
than a whole lap of a median block and **cannot exist**. We reached **2.9x**; ~3.3x is the hard
limit at `ARC_MAXF` 0.90. **To go further a route must cross a street onto the next block** — new
machinery, not a knob.

`walkFit` trims a route that does not fit instead of throwing the **mover** away, and demotes a
lapper whose ring is blocked to ping-pong. It **never fires today** — 0 of 2,790 routes need it,
every ring here is walkable — but `CITY_STYLE` (SLUMS / WIDE / DOWNTOWN) changes block sizes, so it
is kept, and it is proved by 9 checks against a stubbed `cityGround` rather than left as code that
has never run.

### The reasons are parameters

`REASONS` is a weight, a speed multiplier and a pause multiplier, so adding one is a line: POLICE
(lap, slow), DELIVERY, REMOVAL, MILITARY (fast), SOCIAL (his joke, kept — two robots going nowhere
slowly is free character). Verified to appear at their weights over 2,790 routes.

**Not built**, each needing art or paired movement rather than a knob: a carried box for DELIVERY,
two sentries carrying one crate for REMOVAL, a file of three for MILITARY, a pair for SOCIAL. The
schedule already supports them — give two movers the same route and a phase offset.

### Density

`MEAN` 5 / `SD` 2.0 / `MAX` 16 → **132 movers against 141 standers, a true half-and-half.** Doubling
`MEAN` meant moving all three: `SD` is in the same units (the `FOE_POP.SD` trap) and `MAX` 8 would
have capped it, since slack starts at `MEAN + 3*SD` = 11.

### Cost

`updateFoes` **0.063 ms**, placement **0.265 ms**, of a 16.7 ms frame, with 164 standers + 162
movers. The 98 movers are the cheap half at ~0.02 ms. Worst case (everyone provoked) is
self-limiting at ~45, because `foeThink` un-provokes anything past `GIVE_UP` 900 — so
`foeSeparate`'s O(n²) cannot blow up by raising `MEAN`.

### Three real bugs

1. **`citySentries` was culling movers.** It culls anything with an `f.key` and movers have one, so
   it removed them and deleted their keys from the **standing** index — **36 of 97 movers
   permanently orphaned**. Same trap as `clearFoes` vs `foeLive`. Both sites now skip `f.route`.
2. **The rejoin never converged.** It walked back at working pace toward a point that itself moves
   at up to 1.45x that — 140 units adrift after ten seconds, for ever. It now hurries at
   `CHASE_SPD`, with `REJOIN_T` as a backstop. **`CHASE_SPD` must stay above the fastest mover** or
   this breaks again; that is a check.
3. **`SHRINK` fractions compounded** (1, 0.72, 0.36, 0.12…) instead of applying to the original.

### Two false alarms of mine, both from bad measurement

- I reported movers dropping 132 → 102 from longer routes being rejected. That was a **four-spot
  average against a single spot**. The real figure is 132 either way, no loss.
- Two test failures were my own premises: one measured a **detached object** after its body was
  culled past `DROP`, the other assumed a mover would give up while it was actually **closing** on a
  stationary walker at 52 u/s with `GIVE_UP` at 900. Park the walker at a *measured* distance —
  over `GIVE_UP`, under `DROP`.

### Harness traps, both of which cost time

- **`last = performance.now()` is the real clock and `headless.js` ticks from `t = 0`.** If the match
  is already in `state === 'play'` on the **first** tick, `dt` is about **-1.8e9 s**, `acc` goes
  permanently negative and **`step()` never runs again** — the game looks alive and simulates
  nothing. **Tick once before starting the match.** `selftest_foe.js` only gets away with it because
  earlier heads tick while the state is `title`.
- In the city **`keys['ArrowUp']` drives north, back out through the gate**, and `toggleOnFoot()`
  leaves the walker at the origin. A 40 s "soak in the city" was measuring empty wasteland. Pin the
  position, face `-Math.PI/2`, and assert the sentry count mid-run.

`node tools/selftest_foe.js` — **150 checks**.

## 2026-11-10 — The sentry's first-person sprite: `FOE_VSC`

**He played it and said the sentries were "a squaredy mess".** They were, and nothing was wrong
with the art.

`rasterCar`'s `vsc` is **pixels per world unit**. `bakeFoeSheet` passed **1**, so the 22-unit
sentry baked into a **26-pixel** sprite and was then magnified by distance:

| distance | drawn | magnification of a 26 px sheet |
|---|---|---|
| 200 u | 16 px | none — the sheet was bigger than needed |
| 120 u | 27 px | x1.0 |
| 60 u | 54 px | x2.1 |
| 25 u | 129 px | x5.0 |
| **18 u** | **179 px** | **x6.9** — and 18 is `FOE_AI.STOP`, where a provoked sentry stops in your face |

So it looked worst exactly when it mattered most. Over 15 s of ordinary walking it was **never**
magnified (biggest draw 16 px, 0 of 13,256 draws) — at street distance the blockiness is the
**pane**, not the sprite, and no amount of sheet resolution touches it.

`sentry@96` is voxelised at res 96 and the bake was throwing roughly three quarters of it away.
**No new art, no re-voxelising** — the same model rasterised finer. `FOE_VSC = 4` makes a 103 px
frame, which *is* the model's native resolution, so 4 is the ceiling rather than a preference.

**The change is three lines:** `bakeFoeSheet` passes `FOE_VSC`; `drawFoeBillboard` divides it
back out (`const k = scale/sp.vsc`); the sheet cache keys on `FOE_VSC` so turning the knob
rebuilds instead of silently keeping the old sheet. `bakeFoe2D` is untouched — the driving pane
already bakes at `S` and divides by `zk = S/sp.S`, which is why it always looked better.

### The bug this nearly shipped with

`foeSheetNew` padded the frame `+2` and anchored at `fh - 1 - R*ky*vsc`, **both in sheet pixels**.
Invisible while `vsc` was always 1 — but the billboard divides `sp.vsc` back out, so a flat margin
shrinks with it and the sentry would have **floated about 1 world unit off the ground**, 14 px
adrift at arm's length. Margin and anchor now scale with `vsc`. Verified against the old vsc-1
geometry across billboard scales 0.5 → 8: worst difference 0.25 px at normal range, 2 px with a
sentry filling the screen, all of it `Math.ceil` rounding — and *finer* at vsc 4, because the frame
now rounds in quarter-units instead of whole ones. **At vsc 1 the new formula is byte-identical to
the old**, so nothing else built on `foeSheetNew` moved.

### What it costs

| vsc | frame | sheet | memory |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (before) | 15x26 | 240x156 | 0.14 MB |
| 3 | 44x78 | 704x468 | 1.26 MB |
| **4 (now)** | **59x103** | **944x618** | **2.23 MB** |

All of it **one-off and shared by every sentry alive** — the sheet is keyed by model name, so 274
of them cost what one does — and the bake is **lazy per pose** (`foeSheetEnsure`), so it arrives
spread over the first six poses rather than as a hitch.

**Per frame it costs nothing extra.** The blit runs with `imageSmoothingEnabled = false`, so it is
destination-bound, and the destination size does not change. The only source-sized per-frame path
is `hitFrame` (the hurt flash), 2,960 px at vsc 3 against 5,141 at vsc 4, for 0.12 s after a hit.
And the frustum cull means **max 16 billboard draws a frame** at 221 live sentries.

**It is paid whether or not ASCII is on.** `asciiBlit` is a post-process that reads the finished
pane back (index.html ~4806), so the billboard is drawn identically either way — **G** only decides
whether the detail survives. Dropping `FOE_VSC` to 3 while in ASCII would be a real saving if the
memory ever matters.

**Not measured:** actual bake milliseconds. Headless stubs the canvas and Chrome's virtual time
freezes `performance.now()`, so screenshot runs report 0 ms. Use the viewer's **time bake** button
in a real browser, or dev mode (**I**) in game for fps.

Proof: `.shots/game-vsc4.png` (in game, 34 units, ASCII off — both optic lenses readable) against
`.shots/vsc-1.png` (the old smear).

### The cars got the same treatment — `CAR_VSC`, but only with ASCII off

`bakeFpsSprites` rastered at `vsc = 1` too, and a car magnifies **harder** than a sentry because you
can walk right up to one: x1.4 at 150 units, x2.3 at 80, x4.5 at 40, **x9.0 at 20**.

Same three-line shape: the frame and the raster both take `vsc`, the draw divides it back out
(`s3 = scale*(onFoot ? 1 : carMag(perp))/sp.vsc`), and the sheet key carries the vsc. At vsc 1 it is
byte-identical to before; worst drawn-rect difference is 0.33 px at normal range, 3 px with a car
filling the screen — all `Math.round(fh*0.62)` rounding.

**But a car sheet is not the sentry's.** The sentry has ONE shared sheet (2.23 MB, 274 of them cost
what one does). Cars are keyed by **mesh and colour**, so there is one per livery, and the frame is
`ceil(52 * c.scale * vsc)` with `c.scale` running to 1.5 in play. Measured on foot, 7 cars, 4
distinct sheets:

| vsc | frames | total |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (before) | 52/78/42/79 | **4.1 MB** |
| 2 | 104/156/83/157 | 16.3 MB |
| **3 (now)** | 156/233/124/235 | **36.4 MB** |
| 4 | 208/311/165/313 | 64.7 MB |

So vsc 4 is cheap for the sentry and 65 MB for the cars. **`CAR_VSC = 3`.**

**His idea, and it is the right one: only pay it with ASCII off.** `carVsc()` returns `CAR_VSC` when
`asciiMode === 0` and **1** otherwise, because `asciiBlit` quantises the pane into glyphs and the
detail cannot survive it. Pressing **G** calls `carSheetsReset()`, which clears `FPS_SHEETS` and
nulls every `c.fps`, so only one set is ever resident — 36.4 MB with ASCII off, **1.5 MB with it
on**. The cost is a rebake on the keypress, which is the right trade.

Unlike the sentry's, a car sheet bakes **all 64 frames at once** (the sentry's is lazy per pose), so
that hitch is real.

**The obvious next win, deliberately not done:** a car mesh is 35.0 x 18.2 x 11.0, projecting to
about **35x14 inside a 52x52 cell** — roughly 4.6x more sheet than the car needs. Sizing the cell
from the mesh the way `foeSheetNew` does for a figure would make vsc 4 cost less than vsc 2 does
now. It is not a free change: `ay = round(fh*0.62)` **is** the contact patch the wheels stand on, so
the anchor has to be rederived from the mesh at the same time or every car floats.

Two things noticed in passing, both pre-existing and **not** touched:
- The sheet key is `mesh|colour` but **not `c.scale`**, while the frame size depends on scale. Two
  cars of the same mesh and colour at different scales share whichever sheet was baked first.
- `c.scale` changes the frame size but `rasterCar` was called with a fixed `vsc` of 1, so scaling a
  car added padding rather than making the car bigger in its cell.

Proof: `.shots/car-vsc3.png`.

## 2026-11-10 — NEXT: the demo is three city quests

**This is the demo.** The city is currently an endless free drive with sentries in it and no
reason to go anywhere. Three quests give it one. Nothing here is built yet; the shape and the
story are settled, the details marked OPEN are not.

### The canon this rests on — read before changing any of it

`assets/comic/COMIC_SCRIPT.md` and the 2026-11-06 story entry are the source. Short version:

- Everyone is **nameless**.
- **HIM:** a mechanic robot, an older model. A welding visor flipped up on his head, a leather
  apron, one rusted arm and one chrome. He built the war-car from scrap.
- **HER:** sleeker, newer, with **a square socket on her chest where an old data chip glows
  cyan**. The chip carries code the city needs. The **Collectors** (the armoured hunter cars)
  took her and keep her **intact**, because they don't know whether they can get the code out
  without breaking her.
- **The techno-lords** rule the neon city behind its walls. Everything that didn't make the cut
  was left outside, in the **Scrapheap**.
- **The Scrap Saints** are the rebels — they fight the lords, *or whoever controls them*. They
  don't care which; they pay bounty for any state property you wreck. Their line:
  *"We don't care who's holding the leash. We cut the leash."*
- Old models are beneath the guards' notice. The guards **only fear high-tech androids packed
  with powerful energy attacks** — that is the in-world reason the sentries ignore you.

**New (2026-11-10): her memory has been flashed.** She is intact, but she does not remember who
she is or what she carries. The chip is still in the socket — they daren't pull it. So rescuing
her does **not** answer anything, and "retrieve her memory" is a later quest, not this demo.

### Nobody knows what the techno-lords are. That is the point.

Three claims circulate in the city, and **the game never settles it**:

1. **The AI took power.** It overthrew the humans and rules in their place.
2. **The humans are still there.** The lords are a front; the hand is behind the curtain.
3. **The robots are their own faction now,** at war with humans because *the humans tried to
   destroy the robots first*.

Each claim explains the one fact the player has already *played* — "they hunt only new tech" —
a different way: the regime fears being outclassed / the humans fear a machine that could
refuse / the state is **conscripting** the best machines for a war that never ended.

The mechanic came first and the lore explains it three ways. Keep it that way: no quest resolves
it, and each one yields a single hard fact that fits more than one claim.

Cheap way to put the argument in the world: **graffiti on the building walls**, one slogan per
claim, placed by the same deterministic per-chunk trick as the sentries. The player reads the
argument off the city and no character has to explain it. The sentries never say what they
believe — they just stand there and let you walk past.

### The quest shape (decided)

**Radio briefs you; two locations per quest; gear first, job second.**

- **The radio gives the quest.** No dialogue system, no interiors, no talking heads needed — and
  it talks **while you drive**, which turns travel into the story beat instead of dead air. An NPC
  in a room can't follow you.
- **Location 1 is where the GEAR is.** Not a talker. The reason you must go is that the *object*
  is there. A location whose only content is text is the oldest bad quest design there is.
- **Location 2 is the job.**
- **PRACTICAL RULE: the gear site sits SHALLOWER than the target** (smaller `|y|`), so a quest is
  one continuous push deeper into the city and never a backtrack over ground you've cleared.
  This is just a number per site, so it is free — don't get it wrong anyway.
- The radio **drops a marker on the map** (`M`, `MAP_RADII`, city overview already exist). That is
  the objective UI, and it is diegetic rather than a HUD arrow.

**He is a mechanic.** Every gear job fits his profession — he cuts, he assembles, he drives. Use
that instead of inventing specialists.

### Quest 1 — RESCUE HER  (OPEN: the least decided of the three)

- **Nobody briefs you.** This one is not offered. The Saints would rather you hit the datacenter;
  the old model would rather you seal the bunker. You go anyway — it is the comic's last line,
  *"Hold on. I'm coming."*, and it is the only quest that defies both factions.
- **Location 2:** wherever they are holding her. OPEN.
- **The escape is the design.** The whole game teaches that nobody looks at you. The moment she is
  on your back **you are carrying new tech** and every sentry in the city can see you. The way out
  is the way in with the rule inverted — and that is `f.mad` / `foeMadN`, code that already ships.
  *"They never looked at me twice. They looked at her the whole way out."*
- **Gear — OPEN, and note the trap:** he already has a welding visor, so a cutting torch is not
  something he needs to buy. The gear has to be something he cannot improvise. Candidates: a
  **Collector transponder** so the grabbing arms read him as one of theirs; a **power cell**
  because the holding block is shielded and his torch won't bite; a **blank memory core** to catch
  whatever is left of her (which comes back empty — that is the hook for the later memory quest).
- **Fact it yields:** she is out, and she does not know who she is. She says one thing that fits
  none of the three claims cleanly, because she is the only one who has been inside.

### Quest 2 — THE DATACENTER  (a fight in a huge city square)

> **SUPERSEDED 2026-11-17 — do not build from this.** The datacenter is now an
> infiltration: one hardware payload, three floors of braided labyrinth, the plant as the
> boss fight, and the heat as the escape. The square android and the three-part bomb are
> dropped. See *Quest 2 — THE DATACENTER: the worm, three floors, and the heat* at the top
> of this file. Kept here for the reasoning only.

- **The Saints brief you** over the radio. It is their war aim: if claim 1 is true, the usurper
  lives in the machine.
- **Location 2 is a huge city square** — a plaza where the lords' physical plant is exposed, and
  **you have to fight something there**. Not a stealth bomb run.
- **The canon already wrote the boss.** The guards "only fear high-tech androids packed with
  powerful energy attacks". That is exactly what should be standing in the square: the thing even
  the lords' own guards are afraid of. The player has spent the whole city being ignored; here is
  the one machine that does not ignore him.
- **Location 1 — the supplier:** explosives **assembled** from parts bought separately (fuel,
  oxidiser, detonator). A bomb built from three purchases feels earned in a way a bought bomb
  doesn't, and the assembly is a mechanic's job. Reuses the store screen and `cash`.
- **Fact it yields:** the datacenter dies and **something keeps giving orders anyway.**

### Quest 3 — CEMENT THE BUNKER  (the best-shaped one — build it when you want a set-piece)

- **The radio: "there is a hidden bunker in the city, and we just got intel on that."** The intel
  comes from **an old model, and he is telling the truth** — he was there, he saw it. Read him
  narrowly: he is reliable **about the bunker**, not omniscient about who runs the state. "A human
  is alive in a hole" and "humans are still running things" are different statements. He knows the
  first. Nobody knows the second.
- **Location 1 is a yard, and you STEAL A CONCRETE MIXER.** This is the best first location in the
  game because it is not a waypoint, it is a transformation: you arrive in the war-car and leave
  driving a slow, heavy truck. It is the only quest that changes how driving *feels*.
- **Location 2:** the bunker's air intake. You back the mixer up and **pour**.
- **You do not kill the last human.** You seal him in and drive away. Very robot, and the best of
  the three endings: *he wanted the world to wait for him. Now it can.*
- **Fact it yields:** the air handlers were **already running on a timer** — nobody inside has
  touched anything for decades.
- **Cost: this is the expensive one.** A second drivable vehicle with its own handling.

### The chirp language (his idea, 2026-11-10)

Dialogue should **sound** like the machine-to-machine language — R2-D2-ish chirps — while the
text types out. This is nearly free and it fits machinery that already exists:

- The comic **already types captions** at `COMIC.TYPE_SPEED` letters/second. Fire one chirp per
  revealed character and the speech paces itself off the existing typewriter.
- `Snd` is fully synthesised WebAudio with `blip(freq, dur, type, vol, slide)` and
  `noise(dur, vol, cutStart, cutEnd)` already in hand. Derive the pitch from the character code so
  a given line always chirps the same way, add a slide for the warble, and skip the chirp on
  spaces and punctuation so it phrases.
- Give each speaker a pitch band and a waveform so the Saints, the old model and the radio are
  distinguishable without a portrait. She should sound different from everyone.

### Engineering notes

- **The city has no unique buildings at all.** It is infinite and procedural (`makeCityChunk`,
  `cityRng`, `hash3`). Placing sites is the **same problem as the sentries**, and `foeChunkPlan` is
  the template: deterministic, keyed, materialised within a radius.
- The one difference: sites are **unique and must appear exactly once at a known depth**, so they
  want a fixed table — `CITY_SITES = [{kind, atY, ...}]` — not a random draw.
- **`makeCityChunk` has to reserve a `core`** for a site, or a procedural building will grow on
  top of it. This is the first real change to the city generator.
- "Drive deeper to reach your objective" also gives the city the **progression it currently
  lacks**. Gear sites shallow, targets deep (the practical rule above).
- The radio wants a tiny queue: a line, a speaker, a chirp band, and a map marker.

### Before building any of it — test the traversal

More locations is only better if **crossing the city is already fun**. Drive 2,000 units across it
and ask whether you'd want to do that twice per quest. If the answer is no, the fix is in the city,
not in the quest structure, and two locations per quest would make it worse. Ten-minute test; do it
first.

- The rescue: where she is held, and what the gear is (see the trap above).
- What is standing in the square, in detail.
- ~~Whether the three quests are pick-one-per-run or all three in one run.~~ **DECIDED
  (2026-11-10): pick ONE per run — but the other two quests' LOCATIONS still stand in the city.**
  You drive past the datacenter square and the mixer yard whether or not you took that job, so the

- Whether the graffiti ships with the demo or later.

## 2026-11-10 — Camera shake fell off a cliff; sentry density settled at MEAN 5

Two things, both from winding the sentry density up and then back down.

### The shake bug: `explode()` had no distance term, and never had

He played it and reported "the screen is shaking a lot". `explode()` is the only thing that kicks
the camera, and it did so at **full strength from anywhere in the world**:

```js
cam.shake = Math.min(9, cam.shake + power*3);   // no distance term at all
```

That was invisible as a bug for the whole life of the game, because explosions were rare and
always happened under your own bumper. The sentries broke it: `foeDie` calls `explode` and hunter
cars cruise at `CITY_CRUISE` 115, well over `FOE.RAM_MIN` 40, so **they mow sentries down all over
the city** — and the 2D pane only shows ±160 × ±90 world units, so almost none of it is on screen.

Measured at MEAN 25, driving and **never firing a shot**: 3 deaths in 30 s, **all three further
than 600 units away**, screen shaking 13% of frames. Firing: 27 deaths, 25 off screen, 27% of
frames, average `cam.shake` 0.89 and pegged near the cap of 9 two thirds of the time.

A sentry's blast was also rated `1.2` — **identical to a whole car exploding** (barrel 0.8,
missile 1.0, mine 1.1, car 1.2, car bomb 2.4).

**The fix** — `SHAKE` in config.js §7b, and `shakeFade(x, y)` in index.html:

- Full kick inside `SHAKE.NEAR` 110, linear to **zero at `SHAKE.FAR` 700**.
- `SHAKE.SENTRY` 0.45 instead of 1.2. One person popping is a bump (1.35 kick, 0.1 s), not a bomb.
- A blast **beside** you is untouched: car bomb at your feet still 7.20, car wreck still 3.60.

After: 4% of frames while driving, 9% while firing — in a run that had **more** deaths (35 vs 3;
hunter AI varies a lot run to run, so compare rates, not single runs).

### The booms too (his call: "if it is offscreen don't add the boom sfx")

`Snd.boom()` takes no arguments — one fixed-volume bang regardless of distance — so it is gated on
the **same** `shakeFade`, which keeps exactly one definition of "too far to matter" for both the
kick and the bang. Past `SHAKE.FAR` you get neither.

Safe because **nothing the player fires can reach that far**: a missile is 190 units/s for 2.6 s
= **494 units**, mines land behind you, barrels are beside you. So it can only ever silence an
explosion someone else caused, off screen. **That bound is now a test** — raise missile range past
`SHAKE.FAR` and the suite fails instead of the game quietly going deaf.

### Density: settled at MEAN 5 (and the city-centre set is on record)

Swept to 10× and back. **Every number in `FOE_POP` has to move together** — this is the trap:

- `SD` is in the **same units as MEAN**. At MEAN 2.5, SD 0.8 is a 33% spread and blocks really
  differ; leave SD at 0.8 and raise MEAN to 25 and it is a **3% spread** — every block gets exactly
  25 and "some blocks busy, some empty" is gone, replaced by a flat rate.
- `MAX` is a ceiling, not a second knob, and only does something while it is being hit. It stops
  mattering past about `MEAN + 3*SD`; below that it quietly caps MEAN.
- **`ROADSIDE` moves OPPOSITE to MEAN.** Low MEAN needs it high or you meet nobody (0.6 at MEAN
  2.5); high MEAN needs it low or the frontage packs shoulder to shoulder and the city reads as a
  guard of honour lining the route. Compare `.shots/extreme-mean-25.png` (0.6) with
  `.shots/extreme-roadside-0.2.png` (0.2) — same 377 sentries, completely different city.
- New: **`FOE_POP.GAP` 16**. At MEAN 25 sentries were being placed **inside each other** (closest
  pair 0 units; already only 4 at MEAN 2.5, because `foeSeparate` only separates *provoked* ones).
  `foeSpot` now rejects a spot too near an already-placed one, which also makes density
  **self-limiting**: a block that runs out of room just skips that sentry.

**Now: `MEAN 5, SD 1.6, MAX 12, ROADSIDE 0.45`** → 148 live, nearest 200, 14.3 within 600,
28 in a good look.

**The city-centre set, measured and kept** for when the centre gets its own density:
`MEAN 25, SD 8, MAX 60, ROADSIDE 0.2` → ~377 live, ~190 in one view, nearest ~180, **0.28 ms of a
16.7 ms frame**. CPU is not what breaks at 10×; the distribution and the overlaps are.

Both sweep tables live in comments beside the knobs in config.js, with a note that rows are only
comparable **at equal `ROADSIDE`** — the MEAN 5 row has twice the bodies of the MEAN 3.5 row but
*fewer* within 300 units, purely because a smaller share stands on the frontage.

### Tests and one harness trap worth knowing

`node tools/selftest_foe.js` — **120 checks** (108 before; 12 new across the shake and the booms).
Plus `selftest_boost.js` 30 and `selftest_pad.js` 60.

**The harness trap:** `last = performance.now()` is the real clock, and `headless.js` ticks from
`t = 0`. If the match is already in `state === 'play'` on the **first** tick, `dt` comes out at
about **−1.8e9 seconds**, `acc` goes permanently negative and **`step()` never runs again** — the
game looks alive and simulates nothing. **Tick once before starting the match** (the title branch
returns early and resets `last`). `selftest_foe.js` gets away with it only because earlier heads
tick while the state is still `title`.

Second trap, same session: in the city, `keys['ArrowUp']` drove the car **north, back out through
the gate**, and `toggleOnFoot()` left the walker at the origin — 14,027 units from the nearest
sentry. A 40 s "soak in the city" was measuring empty wasteland. Pin the position and face
`-Math.PI/2`, and assert the sentry count mid-run.

## 2026-11-09 — Sentries in the city, with their own AI

**Start here.** The plan below is what this was built from; everything under that is older and
done. Built, checked, and **playtested — which is the only reason it works**: the first cut
passed 103 checks and put seventeen sentries in the city that the player could not see at all.
Read *the one that got away* before touching the placement.

### The rules, as decided. Do not re-litigate these.

| rule | where it lives |
|---|---|
| scattered all over the city, by density, not spawned at a point | `citySentries` / `foeSeedChunk`, foe.js |
| each one is on its own; shooting one does not turn the others on you | `foeDamage` touches only `f` |
| they ignore you until you shoot them (the comic's rule) | `foeThink` returns at once unless `f.mad` |
| being shot is what provokes one — that one | `foeDamage`, and **only when `ownerId === 0`** |
| a provoked one FOLLOWS you and **never shoots** | `foeThink`; there is no foe weapon anywhere |
| five of your pistol shots kill one | `FOE.HP` 90 against `PISTOL.DMG` 18 |
| the car's weapons kill one too | `updateShots`, `explode`, `foeRam` |
| it gives up when you get far enough away | `FOE_AI.GIVE_UP` + `LOSE_T` |

**The open question was asked and answered: it never shoots.** The brief said "they just follow
you for now" and a later answer said it chases the car "and shoots it as well"; asked which, the
user chose **follows only, never shoots — in the car or out of it**. So there is no sentry
weapon in the code at all, and adding one is a new decision, not a gap to be filled in.

### Where the knobs are

**`config.js` §3e** — two blocks, both new:

- **`FOE_AI`** — `CHASE_SPD 52` (he walks 46, runs 92, so a sentry out-paces a stroll and loses a
  sprint — that is the whole reason `GIVE_UP` means anything), `TURN 3.2`, `NEAR 170`, `STOP 18`,
  `GIVE_UP 900`, `LOSE_T 1.2`, `STUCK_T/STUCK_D`.
- **`FOE_POP`** — **superseded: see the 2026-11-10 density entry for the settled values.** As
  first built: `MEAN 0.22` sentries per block, `SD 0.80`, `MAX 3`, `SEED 4231`, `KEEP 3000`,
  `DROP 4200`, `TRIES 8` (no `GAP` yet).

**`foe.js`** — `FOE.HP 90`, `FOE.HIT_R 8` (the pistol's tolerance on a sentry; `PISTOL.HIT_R` 14
is a car's number), `FOE.SHOT_R 9`, `FOE.RAM 0.9` / `RAM_MIN 40`, `FOE_HIT_LIFT 0.5`.

### The one that got away: they were there, and they were invisible

Worth more than the rest of this entry, because every check passed and the feature did not work.
The user playtested it and said: *"I tried both index and the gunlab and I see no sentries."*

**What was wrong, in two layers.**

1. **The live set was a band of chunks, not a radius.** A chunk is 1400 long but the city's whole
   **4000-unit width**. The player only ever occupies the highway corridor at x≈0, so giving a
   body to a whole chunk spent **20 of 26 sentries three to four thousand units out to the side**,
   in blocks nobody will ever walk down. 5 of 26 were inside the 1500-unit billboard cull; **1**
   was inside 600 units.
2. **And the deeper one: a block has four pavement edges, and only ONE faces the highway.**
   `foeSpot` picked uniformly, so three sentries in four stood on interior side streets. This is
   why the obvious fix did not work — **raising the density changed nothing**. Measured:
   `MEAN 0.22` → 7 live, 1 inside 600; `MEAN 0.8` → 10 live, **still 1 inside 600**. The highway
   is a 360-wide corridor with no blocks in it and only one or two blocks within 600 units at all,
   so per-block density cannot put anybody near the road however high you wind it.

**The fix is `FOE_POP.ROADSIDE` (0.6)** — that share stand on their block's road-facing edge, the
rest keep to the back streets so it does not read as a guard of honour lining the route — plus
`KEEP`/`DROP` as a **radius** (1800/2400) instead of a chunk band. Nearest sentry on arrival went
from 485 units to **183**, and sentries met on a 32-second walk from **1 to 6**.

**Three lessons, in the order they cost time:**

- **"It exists in the array" is not "it is in the game."** Every check passed because every check
  asked whether the sentry was *placed, drawn, and culled correctly*. None asked whether the
  player would ever be near one. There are now three that do, under *the regression that bit* —
  nearest-under-400 and the road-facing share. If those fail, the enemies are unmeetable again.
- **A screenshot with the subject teleported in front of the camera proves nothing.** The original
  shots moved the nearest sentry to 78 units to get a good picture of it. That is the right shot
  for judging the *art* and worthless for judging the *placement*, and it is what let this ship.
  The honest shot is `shots/real-arrival.png`: stand where the player stands, face the heading
  with the most sentries in view, move nothing.
- **Two false trails, both mine, both from bad instrumentation.** A "the hunters are mowing them
  down" theory came from a diagnostic whose car was still moving — it wrecked itself and I spent
  the run measuring a dead game. A "the barrier occludes them" theory came from reading a
  screenshot instead of testing: at 200 units a sentry draws 16 px in a 180-tall pane, barrier or
  no barrier. Both were disproved in one command each. Test the mechanism, do not infer it.

### How many there actually are — measured, not guessed

**`FOE_POP.MEAN` is the knob** — the only number that changes how many there are. Swept along
the road and averaged over four spots (`live` = bodies inside `KEEP`, `view` = most visible in
one first-person look). The same table is in config.js beside the knob:

| MEAN | MAX | live | nearest | <300 | <600 | in one view |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.4 | 3 | 15 | 256 | 1.3 | 2.3 | 7 |
| 0.8 | 3 | 20 | 293 | 1.3 | 3.0 | 8 |
| 1.2 | 4 | 24 | 277 | 0.8 | 2.8 | 11 |
| **2.5** | **6** | **60** | **249** | **3.3** | **8.0** | **23** | *(set here)* |
| 3.5 | 8 | 80 | 190 | 5.0 | 11.0 | 28 |

**Two things the table will not tell you unless you look at it.** Below about **1.2 the curve is
flat and noisy** — at those rates the handful of blocks near the road dominate and luck decides,
which is why 0.4 and 0.8 both give ~1 sentry inside 300 units. And **`MAX` is the real ceiling**:
leave it at 3 and winding `MEAN` past ~1.5 does nothing, because every block clamps and the knob
stops answering. Raise them together.

`ROADSIDE` is the other half and is not interchangeable with `MEAN`: it decides *whether you ever
meet* the sentries, where `MEAN` only decides how many exist. Winding `MEAN` up with `ROADSIDE`
at 0 just fills the back streets — that is exactly the failure above. The clamp at nought also
lifts the realised rate above `MEAN`, since the normal's whole left half lands on "none".

**Cost at MEAN 2.5** (41 alive at the arrival spot), out of a 16.7 ms frame: `updateFoes` 0.004,
`citySentries` 0.014, `foeRam` 0.0005, `gatherBillboards` 0.019 — **0.04 ms the lot**. Even the
pathological case of every sentry provoked at once is 1.5 ms. Density is not what will cost
frames here; the per-sentry work is all O(n) over small n and the sheets are shared, so count is
very nearly free.

`devMode` (I) prints `SENTRIES n` — and `ON YOU n` in red when any are provoked — on the on-foot
HUD, so all of this can be turned by looking rather than by guessing.

### What is reused, and the one thing that is not

- **The flow field is the hunters' own** (`FLOW`/`flowNext`), so a sentry comes down the street
  that leads to you instead of grinding into a wall. It is built with *car* clearances; the only
  cost of borrowing it is that a sentry keeps to the roadway. A pedestrian variant is still a
  later nicety.
- **One line of `cityStep` had to change.** The field was only refilled while a hunter car was
  interested in you, which on foot is never. A provoked sentry hunting you on foot is the one
  thing that needs it with no car to ask — hence `foeMadN`, counted in `updateFoes` and read by
  `cityStep`.
- **Planning and materialising are separate** (`foeChunkPlan` / `citySentries`). The per-block
  normal draw is done once per chunk and cached — deterministic from `(chunk, side)` alone, so
  the same stretch of city always holds the same sentries. What gets a *body* is then decided by
  distance. That split is what lets the scatter stay "all over the city" while the live set stays
  small and near you. `foeLive` is the index of who has a body; `clearFoes` clears it, or the
  spawner would think sentries it can no longer see are still out there and never bring them back.
- **`foeDamage` / `foeDie` / `foeCollide` are NOT `damage()` / its death branch / `walkCollide`.**
  Each says why at its own definition. `damage()` is car-only the whole way down (`untouchable`,
  `armorCut`, respawn, deaths, the pit clip, the bounty); its death branch is the tanque's bomb,
  `lastHitBy`, `frags`, `convoyKills`. `foeDie` takes the reusable core the user asked for —
  `explode()` plus two `particles()` bursts in the sentry's colours — and nothing else.
- **A sentry's own death blast does not chain.** `explode` now has a foes loop, and it reads
  `dmg` **raw** rather than through the cars' old `dmg||30` fallback. Two things turn on that: a
  wrecked sentry passes 0 on purpose and must not splash its neighbour, and a 0-damage hit must
  not *provoke* one either, which would quietly break "no coordination". The cars' line is
  untouched, so nothing about car wrecks changed.

### Four things I did that nobody asked for

Each is small, each is flagged here because it is a decision and not an instruction:

1. **Sentries are solid** (`foeBlock`). A follow-only enemy that you can walk through is not a
   threat, it is a ghost. It does **not** hurt you by touching you — it simply will not let you
   past. Deliberately **not** inside `walkCollide`: `toggleOnFoot` uses `walkCollide` as a "is
   this spot free?" probe for the driver's door, and a sentry loitering by the car would have
   reported `NO ROOM TO OPEN THE DOOR`. There is a check that pins that.
2. **A car that drives into one runs it down** (`foeRam`, `FOE.RAM` 0.9 a unit/s above
   `RAM_MIN` 40, so ~100 u/s is lethal; slower just shoves it clear). A car passing clean
   *through* a sentry reads as a bug, and the game already does this to the player on foot
   (`WALK_RUNOVER`). It applies to hunter cars too, which means the city's own traffic thins
   them out.
3. **The hit flash brightens in the pixel panes** (`hitFrame`'s new `lift`, 0 in ASCII). The
   handoff said to use `hitFrame`'s recolour rather than bake the flash row back, and that was
   right — but a white *tint* through a `color` blend only takes the colour **out**, and a
   sentry is already near-grey, so the flash was all but invisible in pixels. A car never had
   this problem because the pixel panes draw its baked white row and only ASCII recolours. The
   lift is `source-atop` inside the silhouette, and **must stay 0 in ASCII**: `asciiBlit` picks a

## 2026-11-09 — The sentry walks: a rig on the voxel model

**Goal:** a walk cycle without drawing one. The faces are split into groups by where
they sit in the model, each group is rotated about a pivot, and the result is baked like any
other frame. Knobs: **`FOE_RIG` in config.js §3d**. Legs only — the arms are welded to the torso
by the carve, so an "arm" group would drag the chest with it.

**One posing function, both cameras.** `posedMesh(mesh, pose)` returns a new face list; both
`bakeFoeSheet` (first person) and `bakeFoe2D` (top-down) loop over poses and call it. There is no
per-view animation code, and that is the whole reason to pose the mesh rather than the sprite.
A face is assigned to a group by its CENTRE, so a quad never splits across a joint.

**The knee is load-bearing, not decoration.** With straight legs the cycle is a pure scissor:
`sin()` sampled at `POSES` points gives mirror-image duplicates, so 6 poses are really 3 shapes.
Bending the knee **only on the leg swinging forward** (`K*max(0, cos(ph))`) breaks that symmetry.
The selftest asserts all six poses are distinct shapes — that check is what stops someone
"simplifying" the knee away and quietly halving the animation.

**The body's dip is derived, not tuned.** Legs rotating about a hip lift the feet, so the body has
to come down or the sentry walks on tiptoe and bobs the wrong way. Rather than add a BOB constant,
`foeDrop()` puts a foot through the same transform and reads its height off. Pose 0 is exactly the
carve, feet at z=0. What is left is ≤0.65 units of a rotating foot BOX's corner under the road —
0.03 of the model's height, sub-pixel at every size this draws at. Tested, not fixed: an ankle
group would cost a tearing seam for an invisible gain.

**Poses bake lazily, one at a time** (`foeSheetEnsure`), and are **warmed one strip per frame**
once anything is walking (`foeWarm`, called from `updateFoes`). **Note for whoever measures this
next: you cannot time it in headless Chrome** — `--virtual-time-budget` freezes the clock, so
synchronous work always reads 0 ms. The numbers here come from node with the stubbed canvas,
which over-states Chrome; the project's own figure is ~30 ms for a 64-frame car sheet.

### The first-walk stutter, and what it cost (reported from play)

Lazy baking put a **387 ms strip bake on the frame where each pose was first shown** — six times
per view, in the middle of the walk, which is the worst possible moment. The work is
`YAWS x rows x faces` and is vertex-bound (the frames are 15x26 and 23x43; fill is nothing).
Two of those three multipliers were waste:

- **The flash row was dead weight.** Nothing can damage a sentry yet, so half of every bake drew
  a white silhouette that is never shown. Gone - `foeRow` is now one strip per pose. When damage
  arrives, prefer `hitFrame()`-style recolour at draw time, which is what the ASCII pane already
  does for cars, over baking the row back.
- **32 yaws is a car's number.** A car turns constantly and is read at speed; a sentry turns
  rarely and is 9 character rows tall. `FOE_RIG.YAWS` is **16** - 22.5-degree steps, invisible at
  this size.

**387 ms -> 96 ms a strip.** Then `foeWarm` moves the remaining five strips to the frames right
after a sentry starts moving (verified: all six done within six frames) instead of stuttering
through the cycle. If it still bites, the knobs in order of bluntness are `FOE_RIG.YAWS`,
`FOE_RIG.POSES`, and switching `FOE_VOX` to `sentry@64` (2008 faces against 4520).

### A worse bug found while investigating, which was NOT the cause

`bakeFoe2D` keyed its cache on the live `S`. `S` is constant in the city and through the run
stage, which is why this never fired - but **the bend eases the zoom frame by frame**, and there
it would have rebuilt all six strips every frame. `bakeCarSprites` has always avoided this by
pinning `S` to `homeZoom()` and letting `drawCar` scale by `zk`; the foe sheet now does the same,
and a selftest asserts that changing `S` does not rebuild it.

**The phase runs on DISTANCE, not time** (`f.phase += d/FOE_RIG.STRIDE`), so the feet don't skate
when the walk speed changes later. There is no AI: `f.walk` makes a sentry step forward and **Y**
toggles it on every sentry, which is how you look at the rig.

**The weakness, and it is the common case.** Seen side-on or three-quarter the walk reads well.
Seen HEAD-ON it barely moves — legs scissoring fore/aft are almost invisible from the front. That
is physically honest and completely wrong for a game, where the enemy walking straight at you is
what you will look at most. Candidate fixes, none tried: a little lateral sway, a slight lateral
splay on the legs, or arm swing once an enemy is drawn with its arms clear of its body.

**Correction worth keeping:** raising the voxel resolution does NOT separate the arms. The carve
walks each row and finds runs of non-background pixels; arms touching the torso are one run and
get one capped depth, at any resolution. What fixes it is a gap of background in the drawing, or
teaching the carve to split runs at the drawn black outline.

**Verified:** `node tools/selftest_foe.js` → 47/47 (pose 0 is the untouched carve, all poses are
distinct, feet exactly on the ground when standing, nothing sinks, the body dips rather than
rises, phase advances by exactly distance/STRIDE and wraps, the pose index stays inside the
sheet). Chrome: a 6-pose × 4-yaw contact sheet, and six sentries in the lab each frozen at a
different phase.

## 2026-11-09 — One drawing -> a voxel model: sprite_voxelize.py

**Goal:** stop paying for enemy directions in drawings. The cars get 32 angles free because
`bakeFpsSprites` renders them from a mesh; this makes a hand-drawn enemy do the same from ONE
front view. **It works, and the result is honestly worse-looking than the flat sprite** — see the
trade at the end before building on it.

**`tools/sprite_voxelize.py`** — `sentry_idle.png` → `assets/enemies/sentry-vox.js`
(`SPRITEVOX["sentry@64"]`, the same shape `car_voxelize.py` emits, so `voxMesh()` eats it
unchanged). Current build: `--res 64 --round 4.0`, 2008 faces, **68 KB**.

**How it guesses the depth it was never given.** A real visual hull needs two silhouettes to
intersect; with one picture an honest hull is a flat slab. So for every row of the sprite it takes
each horizontal RUN of solid pixels and gives it a superelliptical cross-section of depth
`min(width, cap)`. A narrow run — arm, leg, head — comes out round. A wide run — chest, shoulders
— is capped and comes out a rounded slab. That one rule is most of a humanoid.

**Three things it had to learn the hard way, all still in the code as comments:**

- **The drawn outline became a black skin.** A voxel's side faces take the colour of the column
  they sit behind, so the sprite's bold black outline painted the entire left and right of the
  model black — the first carve was a dark slab from every angle but dead ahead. `--erode 3`
  reassigns `dark` voxels within 3 of the silhouette to the nearest painted role; interior dark
  (the joints) is left alone, because that is real shading.
- **A standing figure gets no light.** `LIGHT` is `(-0.42,-0.58,0.70)`: fine for a car, whose lit
  faces are its horizontal tops, but a standing figure shows the camera its **+y** faces and
  LIGHT's y is negative, so every face you look at bakes at the flat 0.44 ambient. `FOE_LIFT`
  (1.9, a `brightness()` filter on the sheet, not on the flash row) puts it back.
- **Near-white washes out.** `accent` is `hexShade(col, 1.32)`, so a white body clamps accent to
  flat grey and the model loses all hue. `FOE_COL` is `#a8b6d4` — cool and well below white — so
  the highlight stays blue and the amber visor is the only warm thing on the model, which is what
  keeps it winning its ASCII cell.

**Also:** `--smooth 2` majority-filters the roles (the drawing holds far more detail than 36
voxels across can carry, and it aliases into salt-and-pepper), with `lamp` exempt so the visor is
never voted away. `scipy` here is built against numpy 1.x and will not import, so the erosion and
the filter are plain numpy — don't reintroduce the scipy dependency.

**Game side:** `foe.js` gained `foeMesh()`, `bakeFoeSheet()` (32 frames + a flash row, `vsc=1` so
one sheet pixel is one world unit, feet anchored near the frame bottom) and `FOE_RENDER`.
**X toggles voxel ↔ flat picture in place** — that comparison is the whole point of the feature,
keep it. Meshes and sheets are keyed by `SPRITEVOX` name and `spawnFoe(x, y, a, vox)` takes one,
so two carves (or two enemies) can stand side by side; the lab plants `sentry@96` down the left
and `sentry@64` down the right at four distances.

**`bakeFoe2D` / `drawFoe2D`: the same mesh in the DRIVING view.** A billboard only exists inside
the raycaster, so at first a sentry simply vanished when you got back in the car — reported from
play, not caught by a test. The fix is the second payoff of voxelising: the top-down pane is the
same mesh through `rasterCar` at the axonometric `KY`/`KZ` with `vsc = S`, which is what
`bakeCarSheet` does for a car. Baked at `S` and dropped by the same rescale that clears `CAR2D`
(`clearFoeSheets()`), and pushed into the painter's-algorithm `drawables` list on `y`, so sentries
sort correctly against cars and buildings. **The flat sprite could never have done this** — it is
drawn face-on from eye level and has no top-down view at all.

`FOE_LIFT_2D` is 1.4, against 1.9 for first person: the driving camera is only 38° up, so you
still see mostly the figure's vertical faces and only the shoulders and head catch `LIGHT`'s +z.
Unlifted they read as dark smudges beside the car.

**Note for anyone reading a screenshot comparison:** `asciiBlit` is called in exactly two places,
the gunner pane and the walk view. **The top-down driving view is never ASCII.** "Pixel vs ASCII"
is a first-person question only; `G` cycles `asciiMode` 0 → 1 → 2.

**The trade, which is the finding.** Side by side in the lab, at 95 units:

| | flat `sentry_idle.png` | the voxel carve |
|---|---|---|
| looks | crisp, coloured, visor and chest lights read | grey-blue, mushy, washed out |
| turning | always faces you | 32 real angles |
| per enemy | 1 drawing per state × per direction | 1 drawing, ever |
| ASCII | solid `@` block | still reads; slightly busier |

The voxel version **rotates and the flat one cannot**, and that is the only reason to prefer it.
It does not yet look as good, and the gap is tuning, not a wall: `--res`, `--round`, `--smooth`,
`--cap`, `FOE_COL`, `FOE_LIFT`. Worth pushing before committing to a whole roster.

**What it still does not solve:** animation. A voxel model gives angles, not a walk cycle. The
prize is splitting the model into limb groups and posing them before the raster, which is only
possible now that the enemy is 3D — that, not the look, is why this pipeline is worth keeping.

**Verified:** `node tools/selftest_foe.js` → 35/35 (roles are all names `rasterCar` knows, faces
are whole 13-number records, the visor survived as `lamp` faces, the model registers into

## 2026-11-09 — BOOST upgrade: the recharge, not the shove

**Goal:** a fifth thing to buy at the Scrap Saints, for the boost. `BOOST_TIME` also went
**0.9 -> 1.0s** (0.9 was too short to feel). Config: `BOOST_COOL_PER_LEVEL` in **3b**.

**What it does.** `boost` is a fifth upgrade track beside armor / health / gun / missile, and it cuts
the recharge by **1s a level: 10s at level 0, 4s at level 6.**

**Why the recharge and not the strength.** The burn is 1s against a 10s recharge, so at level 0 you
hold a charge **9% of the time** — the cooldown is what limits the boost, by a factor of ten. Halving
it roughly doubles how many boosts a leg gives you, and it is only a timer, so nothing in the physics
moves. Making the boost *stronger* is the trap: `BOOST_ADD` is a target the regulator serves, not a
force, and section 2 of `config.js` already records that a boost which only raises the target spends
itself against the frame's front spring and gains no ground. You would have to raise `BOOST_ROOM`
with it, which pushes the car at the edge of the frame where `WALL_REEL` and the backstop live — the
stuck-car problem all over again. If it ever needs to feel *bigger*, buy `BOOST_TIME` instead:
distance scales linearly with it and it touches no geometry.

**The two places a per-level cooldown goes wrong,** both now covered by the selftest:

- `tryBoost` arming the raw `BOOST_COOL` instead of `boostCool()` — the upgrade would be inert.
- the HUD gauge at the `BST` bar reading `1 - boostCD/BOOST_COOL`. With a 4s countdown over a 10s
  constant the bar fills to 60% and stops, which looks like a broken gauge, not a wrong divisor.

`boostCoolAt(lv)` has a 0.5s floor so a future config edit can't hand out a boost that never ends.
`upgradeTotal()` counts the new track, so the repair price rises with it like the others.

**The store panel grew.** Seven rows: `H` 280 -> 300 and the row pitch 28 -> 26, or CONTINUE sat on
top of the confirm line. `drawOptions()` has an identical-looking panel line — it was left at 280.
The selftest asserts the last row clears the confirm line and the panel clears `VH`, so the next row
added will say so instead of being found in a screenshot.

**Verified:** `node tools/selftest_boost.js` → 30/30 (constants, `tryBoost`, the gauge, a real
60fps recharge timed at ~5s vs ~11s, buying it in the store, the panel geometry). Screenshot of the

## 2026-11-09 — Controller support; MOUSE or CONTROLLER in OPTIONS

**Goal:** play the whole game on a pad. The mouse stays as it is; the player picks one device and
keeps it. New file `pad.js`, knobs in `config.js` **7b (`PAD`)**, new `OPTIONS` screen off the title.

**The layout. Left side is the car, right side is the guns:**

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| left stick | steer (X, analog) + speed trim (Y) | right stick | the turret |
| d-pad | the same four, digital | | |
| L2 | drift (the handbrake) | R2 | missile |
| L1 | boost | R1 | machine gun |
| Square | mine | Cross | confirm · in and out of the car |
| Circle | back | Options | pause |

On foot: left stick walks, right stick looks, L3 runs, R1 is the pistol, Cross gets back in the car.

**Why the buttons landed there.** The two triggers are the two things you **squeeze and hold**: drift
through a corner on the left, the missile on the right. Both guns sit on the right hand, next to the aim
stick, so firing never costs you the aim. Boost is a **tap** and it is a driving input, so it joins drift
on the left shoulder. Mines moved to Square because they are the rarest thing in the game — you carry
2, 3 at most. The user asked for the guns on R1/R2 specifically, having first tried boost on R2.

**Three things worth knowing before changing it:**

- **The device choice settles the aim fight.** `updateTurret` used to take the mouse branch whenever
  `numPlayers===1 && mouseIn`, which in 1P is always — a right stick would have been overridden on
  every frame. There is no last-input-wins arbitration: `padMode()` (pad.js) is the single gate, and
  it wants *both* `inputMode==='pad'` and a pad actually connected. A saved `'pad'` with nothing
  plugged in quietly stays on the mouse, so nobody can lock themselves out of their own game.
- **The turret holds its bearing when the stick is released.** `padAimAngle()` returns `null` inside
  the deadzone and the caller leaves `c.turret` alone. A stick springs back to centre and a mouse
  does not; without this the gun snapped to straight-ahead every time you let go. And because
  `turret` is stored *relative* to the car, the gun keeps pointing where you aimed it while the car
  slides around underneath — that part was free.
- **The aim is screen-faithful, not world-faithful.** The ground is squashed by `KY` (0.62), so the
  stick is read as a direction *on screen* and converted the way the mouse already was: a 45° push
  comes out as 58.2° in world space. Push the stick at the thing you can see and the shot goes there.

**Presses go through `onPress`.** `padTick(dt)` runs once a frame at the top of `frame()` and
synthesises key codes (`'Enter'`, `'Escape'`, `MINE_KEY`, …), so the title, OPTIONS, garage, store,
setup and wreck screens needed no pad code of their own and cannot drift out of step with the
keyboard. Held controls are read where they are used instead. Edges last a whole frame, so more than
one reader can act on the same press.

**Screens answer to the pad even in MOUSE mode** — only the game itself is gated on the chosen
device. This was the other way round at first, and the self-test caught what that meant: someone who
has just plugged a pad in would have to reach for the keyboard to get to OPTIONS and turn the pad
on, which is the one thing they wanted the pad for. `padTick` gates the menu block on
`padConnected()` and everything below it on `padMode()`.

**Also changed:** analog steering is a real gain — `A`/`D` are a flat ±1, so the stick is the first
thing to give proportional cornering. `MENU_TOP` came down 0.62 → 0.56 because OPTIONS made it a
three-item menu and the `driving:` line had landed on top of the `ENTER` prompt. The hint line under
the canvas now follows the chosen device. Split-screen is untouched: in 2P the pad drives for P1 and
leaves the turret to P2's locked mouse rather than fighting over it.

**Verified:** `node tools/selftest_pad.js` — 59 checks on a fake standard-mapping pad, built on
`tools/headless.js`: deadzones and analog diagonals, trigger travel, edge-fires-once, menu repeat,
the KY aim conversion, the turret holding on release, and a full run through title → OPTIONS → a
live run → on foot → back in the car. It also pins the thing most likely to rot — that in MOUSE mode
the cursor still owns the turret and the stick does not touch it. Re-run it after touching input.
Layout checked in Chrome (`--headless=new --screenshot`); `tools/shot.js` is WSL-only and could not
be used here. `tools/headless.js` gained an `Image` stub, which the on-foot pistol's viewmodel needs
before it will draw at all — that gap predates this work.

**Not yet played on a real pad.** The button *indices* assume the W3C standard mapping, which is
what Chrome reports for DualSense/Xbox/8BitDo, but nothing here has been felt by a human. The
deadzones (`PAD.DZ_MOVE` 0.22, `PAD.DZ_AIM` 0.26) and `PAD.LOOK_RATE` 2.6 are first guesses — expect
to move them. The one to watch is `DZ_AIM`: too small and the turret twitches when the stick rests
off-centre, too large and short aim corrections do nothing.

### The hard part: the corner (he expects trouble here, and he is right)

once - at the corner, cars and walls on the *old* leg would suddenly be computed on the new axis.

`PROJECT.md` already holds design notes for exactly this (the deferred **"L-route"**, search for

parametric curve; convoy's `trackProject/trackPoint` are the straight-x special case of it; the
axonometric view is fixed-angle, so **the corner changes which screen axis the action runs
along** - check the corner *reads* on screen before building the rest; verify the path maths
against a hand-computable case in `tools/headless.js` first.

  culling work in (s, lat) and never ask which way is up.

## 2026-11-05 — Raycaster + ASCII performance

Why the walk view is fast, and how to measure it again. Full tables in `PROJECT.md`.

- **ASCII** (`asciiBlit`): was ~9,300 per-cell `drawImage` + two full-screen blend passes =
  29-58 ms/frame. Now built on the CPU into one ImageData (`ascMask`, `ascOut32`) + one
  `putImageData`: ~7 ms. Pixel diff vs old ≤ 2/255.
- **Walls:** `RC_DIRECT` writes walls/windows into `rcPix32` (was ~4,000 `fillRect` + colour
  strings). `RC_OCCLUDE` (`rcOcclude`) culls whole hidden buildings before ray tests (65-72%).
  Both on = **21-43% off a frame**; all variants byte-identical. He ruled out WebGL ("people will
  need a GPU").
- **Correctness checks** to rerun after raycaster changes: rcZ vs brute force over 200 random
  spots (0 bad of 64,000 columns); frame hashes across switch combinations.
- **Benchmark method:** inject a sync `<script>` into a temp copy, flush with
  `ctx.getImageData(0,0,1,1)` per iteration, write results into a `<pre>`, run Windows Chrome
  `--headless=new --dump-dom` (no virtual time), with and without `--disable-gpu`. Compare **ms
  saved**, not totals - headless baselines are bimodal (~6 vs ~15 ms for the same view).
- Gotcha: screenshots at run start can catch the car's invulnerability blink - set
  `cars[0].invuln = 0` in the shot setup.
